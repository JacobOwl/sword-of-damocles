# Dinners as the stage (switch "dinners", bet B5)

Research card for `data/dinners.json` and `src/core/dinner.ts`. Written 2026-11-23 (thread 24).

**What is history and what is invention.** The rule is invention: the couch of honour as a power object, the three parts of the evening, the three ways to mix the wine and what each does, every moment's odds and effects, and the idea that friends from your table plot together. Four moments lean on passages about the elder Dionysius (marked `kind: history`), and the scene is dressed with details from the sources. Each passage below was opened in the saved texts (`sources/` in the Phoenician-Punic World repo, or this repo's own `sources/`) and is quoted as it stands there.

## The four history moments

**If I ruled** (`pretenderTalk`). A political rival at your table talks of what he would do in your place. Plutarch on the elder Dionysius:

> "He used to say, too, that he was on his guard against his friends who were men of sense, because he knew that they would rather be tyrants than subjects of a tyrant." (Plutarch, *Dion* 9; `sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`)

The scene is invention. Plutarch reports a saying, not a dinner.

**The bravest man alive** (`mostValiant`). You ask the table who is the bravest man alive, and a blunt, proud, grim or fiery guest will not say your name. The choice "Let your anger show" is the one marked `history: true`:

> "the man who was angry with Plato because he would not pronounce him the most valiant man alive" (Plutarch, *Dion* 9)

Plutarch gives no setting for this (not even a dinner), and no date. The other man in the story was Plato, who is not in the cast, so a guest takes his place. Plato's visit to the elder Dionysius is usually put after the game's decade. That date is not in the saved texts, so it is unverified here, and the moment, like the Damocles banquet, may come in any season.

**Searched at the door** (`atTheDoor`). An enemy you invited is stopped by your guards. The choice "Strip and search, as for your own brother" is the one marked `history: true`:

> "Neither his brother nor his son could visit him in his apartment wearing any clothes they pleased, but every one had to take off his own apparel before entering and put on another, after the guards had seen him stripped." (Plutarch, *Dion* 9)

Plutarch is describing visits to his rooms, not dinners. Using the search at a dinner is the game's choice.

## Details used to dress the scene (not records)

- **Couches.** Greeks lay on couches at a banquet: "as they reclined on their couches" (Plutarch, *Dion* 23, Dion's banquet for his soldiers at Zacynthus, decades after the game). Damocles is put "on a golden couch covered with a most beautiful woven rug" (Cicero, *Tusculan Disputations* 5.61, Betts; `sources/cicero/tusculan-5-61.excerpts.txt`).
- **Libations, then drinking.** The game's order of the evening (the meal, the libations, the drinking) follows "But after the libations and the customary prayers" (Plutarch, *Dion* 24, the same banquet). One passage is thin support for a fixed order. The three parts are a game structure.
- **Mixing bowls.** Wine was mixed with water in a bowl: "tables and sacrificial meats and mixing-bowls" (Plutarch, *Dion* 29, the street welcome for Dion). **The three strengths (well watered, as usual, strong) and the idea that strong wine loosens tongues are invention.** No saved text gives mixing ratios. The closest passage is about the younger Dionysius, who "plied his mercenaries with strong wine" before a night attack (Plutarch, *Dion* 30).
- **Dinners as politics.** The elder Dionysius used banquets to win people over. After he dissolved an assembly in fear, he won back the crowd "by kindly words, honouring some of them with gifts and inviting some to general banquets" (Diodorus 14.70.3; `sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`). He gave public dinners after his double wedding (Diodorus 14.45.1, already the event "The double wedding"). Those are public feasts for many people. The game's dinner is a small private table, which is invention.

## The fourth history moment: Philoxenus and the quarries

**Your verses** (`yourVerses`, with the follow-up `quarriesPardon`). This is Philoxenus and the quarries. The Greek of Diodorus 15.6 was fetched on 2026-11-23 from the Perseus Digital Library's encoding of Oldfather's Loeb text, through GitHub (CC BY-SA 4.0). It is saved, with its source line, as `sources/diodorus/book15-6.greek.txt` in this repo. No English translation of book 15 could be reached from the cloud. **The English below is the game's own rendering, not checked against a published translation**:

- 15.6.1: the setting. Dionysius, "freed from the wars against the Carthaginians" (ἀπολελυμένος τῶν πρὸς Καρχηδονίους πολέμων), had much peace and leisure and took to writing poems. So the moment comes only while you are not at war with Carthage.
- 15.6.2: the poems are read at a drinking party, "κατὰ τὸ συμπόσιον ἀναγνωσθέντων τῶν τοῦ τυράννου ποιημάτων" ("at the symposium, when the tyrant's poems had been read"). Philoxenus answers too frankly, and Dionysius "προσέταξε τοῖς ὑπηρέταις παραχρῆμα ἀπάγειν εἰς τὰς λατομίας" ("ordered his servants to take him off to the quarries at once"). This is the choice "Have him taken to the quarries", marked `history: true`.
- 15.6.3: "τῇ δʼ ὑστεραίᾳ τῶν φίλων παρακαλούντων συγγνώμην δοῦναι τῷ Φιλοξένῳ" ("the next day, when his friends begged him to pardon Philoxenus") he brought him back to the drinking party. Asked again about the verses, Philoxenus "ἄλλο μὲν οὐδὲν εἶπε, τοὺς δʼ ὑπηρέτας τοῦ Διονυσίου προσκαλεσάμενος ἐκέλευσεν αὑτὸν ἀπαγαγεῖν εἰς τὰς λατομίας" ("said nothing else, but called Dionysius' servants and told them to take him away to the quarries").
- 15.6.4: "μειδιάσας ὁ Διονύσιος ἤνεγκε τὴν παρρησίαν, τοῦ γέλωτος τὴν μέμψιν ἀμβλύνοντος" ("Dionysius smiled and bore the frankness, the laughter blunting the reproach"). This is the choice "Forgive, and bring him back to your table", marked `history: true`.

Philoxenus is not in the cast, so a frank guest (blunt, proud, fiery, shrewd or an outsider) takes his place. Diodorus puts this after the wars with Carthage, which is later than the game's decade. As with the Damocles banquet, the moment comes from conditions (peace), not a date. Diodorus 14.46.6 (saved) names Philoxenus of Cythera among the best dithyramb poets of the time. Wikipedia's paraphrase (`sources/sicily-places/wikipedia-dionysius-i.txt`) agrees with the Greek on the outline.

## Women at the table (a call made without sources)

Wives and sisters of the cast (Aristomache, Theste and others) can be invited like anyone else. **The saved texts say nothing either way** about women reclining at the elder Dionysius' dinners, so this is the game's choice (decided 2026-11-23). If a source turns up, it can change. A source request is in `docs/later.md`.

## Rules (all invention), in brief

- One action, **Give a dinner** (Court): choose the guest of honour (the couch beside yours), 2 to 4 other guests in the city, and the wine (1 silver; 2 if strong). There is no roll.
- **The couch of honour** is held by one man at a time. It adds 1 to his influence while he holds it. The man who held it before loses 1 opinion when you give it to someone else, and he takes a grudge if he is proud, vengeful or a political rival.
- **Three parts of the evening**, each with at most one moment, drawn from the moments that fit the guests. The wine changes the weights: strong wine makes quarrels, slips and your own careless words likelier, and well-watered wine makes old quarrels easier to mend. At most one moment per dinner puts a choice to you, and it appears under "Matters before you".
- **Friendships and quarrels last.** A plot leader recruits a friend from your table first, with +1 on the roll. He never recruits a man he quarrelled with there.
- **Secrets that slip.** With "Secrets and leverage" on, a slip is a named secret. Without it, it is a leverage token, as a successful feast gives.
