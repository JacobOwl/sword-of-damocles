# How offices changed hands at Syracuse (research card)

kind: history, for the redesign of the offices (thread 36, 2026-11-26). Quotes were checked against the saved texts in the Phoenician-Punic World repo (read-only): `sources/diodorus/english/book11.loeb.txt`, `book13.loeb.txt`, `book14.loeb.txt` and `book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt` (Loeb Diodorus, Oldfather), `sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt` (Perrin). Polyaenus is quoted from `docs/research/cast-405-395.md`.

**Dates.** Diodorus counts by Athenian archon years: give ranges. Plutarch's *Dion* is about 357 to 354 BC, fifty years after the game's decade. It shows how Syracusans handled these offices when the city was free, not what happened in 405 to 395.

## Elected and dismissed by the assembly: the generals

- **Dismissed and replaced in one meeting** (406/405): the people "immediately dismissed some of them from office and chose other generals, among whom was also Dionysius" (Diodorus 13.92.1). The cause was a lost war: the Syracusans were blamed because "they elected the kind of leaders through whose fault the whole of Sicily ran the risk of destruction" (13.91.2).
- **Supreme power by acclamation:** "some of the members cried out to appoint him general with supreme power and not to wait until the enemy were storming their walls" (13.94.5). The same assembly remembered Gelon "general with supreme power" at Himera (13.94.5).
- **Regretted at once:** "these men, in their desire to insure their freedom, had unwittingly established a despot over their country" (13.95.2).
- **Fifty years later, the same pattern:** the Syracusans "made Dion and Megacles generals with absolute powers" and "chose twenty colleagues to hold office with them" (Plutarch, *Dion* 29).

## The holder digs in: the generalship becomes hard to remove

- **His own men in the posts:** "He made changes also in the military posts, conferring their commands upon his most faithful followers" (13.96.1).
- **Houses and land:** the houses on the Island went "to his friends and the mercenaries", and the best land "in gifts to his friends as well as to higher officers" (14.7.4 to 14.7.5).
- **An assembly that tried to unseat him, about 396:** the citizens, armed for the war, "talked together about how they took no steps to end their slavery to Dionysius, even though they had an opportunity to depose him" (14.64.4). Theodorus asked in the assembly for him to be "no longer our commander" (14.65.1). It failed when the Spartan admiral backed Dionysius: "the mercenaries flocked about Dionysius, and the Syracusans in dismay made no move" (14.70.2).

## The fleet: voted when free, given by the ruler under a tyranny

- **A tyrant's appointment, taken back at will** (about 390): "he relieved Leptines of his command and appointed Thearides, his other brother, commander of the fleet" (14.102.3). Leptines had been "his admiral" earlier (14.48.4).
- **The admiral lost his office while away:** Hermocrates, sent "as admiral in command of thirty-five triremes", "was overpowered by his political opponents and, upon being condemned to exile, he handed over the fleet" (13.63.1, about 410).
- **Voted by a free assembly, and fought over:** "they held an assembly of their own calling, and chose Heracleides admiral". Dion protested that "he would no longer be general with absolute powers if another should have command of time navy" [sic, the saved text], and "the Syracusans reluctantly revoked the appointment" (Plutarch, *Dion* 33).

## The leader of the people: rises and falls fast

- **No office, only standing:** Diocles was "a most notable leader of the populace" (13.19.4) and "the most influential among them of the leaders of the populace" (13.34.6). He changed the constitution so offices were "chosen by lot" (13.34.6).
- **Exiled in one quarrel:** over the burial of the dead, "Diocles was exiled" (13.75.5, about 408).
- **Magistrates could check a speaker:** "the archons, in accordance with the laws, laid a fine upon Dionysius on the charge of raising an uproar" (13.91.4).

## The citadel and the treasury: changed only by force, betrayal or surrender

- **Built for this purpose:** "a fortified acropolis as a place of refuge in case of immediate need" (14.7.3).
- **Its keeper tempted:** Polyaenus (2nd century AD, anecdotal): "he left Andron in charge of the fortress and the treasury. Hermocrates advised Andron to seize both of these" (5.2.4).
- **Held after the city was lost** (355): it fell only when "provisions failed them, and the mercenaries became mutinous", and the tyrant's son "made terms with Dion" (Plutarch, *Dion* 50).

## The captain of the mercenaries: removed by arrest, with risk

- **Dismissed:** "Dexippus the Lacedaemonian he dismissed to Greece" (13.96.1).
- **Arrested** (about 396): "seeing that the mercenaries were most hostile to him and fearing that they might depose him, first of all arrested Aristotle, their commander". The men then "demanded their pay with some sharpness", and were paid off with Leontini (14.78.1 to 14.78.2).

## The commands of other cities

- **A city's general could hand it over:** "Arcesilaüs, the general of the Catanians, had offered to betray the city to him" (14.15.1).

## A law against the man who rises too far

- **Petalism** (about 454): "the name of the most influential citizen had to be written on an olive leaf", and the man with most leaves went "into exile for five years" (11.87.1). It was "soon repealed" (11.87.3), because the best men kept out of public life (11.87.4). Fifty years before the game; a precedent, not a rule of 405.

## Not found in these texts

- Any term of office for the generals in 405 (yearly is likely for Greek generals but not stated here). The game's yearly election is **invention**.
- Any office in charge of the mint. Dionysius' coin schemes are his own (pseudo-Aristotle, *Economics* 2.2.20, in `docs/research/customs.md`). The mint as a seat is **invention**.
- A named holder of the assembly. The game's assembly seat stands for the leading voice of the people, like Diocles: **invention** built on 13.19.4 and 13.34.6.
