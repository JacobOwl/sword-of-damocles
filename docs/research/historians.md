# The historians of Dionysius' Syracuse: Philistus and Timaeus (card)

kind: history. For the voices sample (`docs/playtests/2026-11-23-voices.md`, bet B6) and for the Phase 4 idea of a biased historian. Every quote was opened in the saved texts in Jacob's Phoenician-Punic World repo (read-only). Both historians' works are lost; we know them through later writers who used and judged them, chiefly Diodorus, Plutarch and Polybius, who had their own leanings. Claims marked **unverified** I have not checked in a saved text.

## Philistus of Syracuse (about 432 to 356 BC, dates from the saved Wikipedia summary): the friend

- **At the start of the tyranny.** When the archons fined Dionysius in the assembly of 406/405, "Philistus, who later composed his History, a man of great wealth, paid the fine and urged Dionysius to speak out whatever he had had in his mind to say" (Diodorus 13.91.4).
- **In the siege of 404.** Against advice to flee, "Philistus, however, who composed his history after these events, declared in opposition to Polyxenus that it was not fitting to dash from the tyranny on a galloping horse but to be cast out, dragged by the leg" (14.8.5).
- **Guard of the citadel, then exile.** Plutarch: "For Philistus at the outset had most zealously assisted in establishing the tyranny, and for a long time was commander of the garrison that guarded the citadel" (*Dion* 11). After a quarrel over a marriage, Dionysius banished him: "Philistus took refuge with some friends in Adria, and there, it would seem, in his leisure, composed the greater part of his history. For he did not return to Syracuse while the elder Dionysius was alive" (*Dion* 11). So the friendly historian wrote much of his work as an exile of the man he praised; how that coloured it is open.
- **His books.** "Philistus ended his first History of Sicily with this year and the capture of Acragas, treating a period of more than eight hundred years in seven Books, and he began his second History where the first leaves off and wrote four Books" (Diodorus 13.103.3, for 406). That the second History covered the elder Dionysius' reign is the usual reading; **unverified** here.
- **His end.** Admiral of the younger Dionysius, killed in a sea battle in 356: "the Syracusans, after they had won the naval battle, dismembered the body of Philistus, dragged it through the whole city, and cast it forth unburied" (Diodorus 16.16.4).
- **How the ancients judged him.** Plutarch, on Ephorus' praise of him: "even he, with all his artifice, cannot extricate himself from the charge of having been the greatest lover of tyrants alive, and more than any one else always an emulous admirer of luxury, power, wealth, and marriage alliances of tyrants" (*Dion* 36). Plutarch's own advice: "he who neither praises the conduct of Philistus, nor gloats insultingly over his misfortunes, takes the fittest course" (*Dion* 36).
- **In the game.** Philistus is one of the court (`data/people.json`). The voices sample lets him write about a game he was in, as the real one did.

## Timaeus of Tauromenium (died about 260 BC): the enemy of tyrants

- **Later, and an exile of a tyrant.** Diodorus on his history of Agathocles: "since he was banished from Sicily by Agathocles and could not strike back while the monarch lived, after his death he defamed him in his history for all time" (21.17.1), and "Timaeus throughout his history incessantly calls him a poltroon and coward" (21.17.2).
- **Against Philistus.** Plutarch: "Timaeus, finding a fair excuse for his animosity in the zeal and fidelity which Philistus showed in behalf of the tyranny, gluts himself with the slanders against him" (*Dion* 36).
- **Polybius' verdict.** "Timaeus makes many untrue statements; and he appears to have done so, not from ignorance, but because his view was distorted by party spirit. When once he has made up his mind to blame or praise, he forgets everything else and outsteps all bounds of propriety" (Polybius 12.7, Shuckburgh).
- **But careful with numbers.** Where Diodorus gives both, Timaeus' Carthaginian armies are smaller than Ephorus': for 396, Ephorus "more than three hundred thousand infantry" (14.54.5), while "Timaeus, on the other hand, says that the troops transported from Libya did not exceed one hundred thousand" (14.54.6); for 406, "a little over one hundred and twenty thousand, according to Timaeus, but three hundred thousand, according to Ephorus" (13.80.5). So "hostile" does not mean careless everywhere.
- **His view of Dionysius I himself.** That Timaeus was hostile to the elder Dionysius is the usual modern reading, and fits his hostility to Philistus and to Agathocles; **unverified** here: no saved passage quotes Timaeus on Dionysius I directly.
- **His standing.** The saved Wikipedia summary quotes Lionel Pearson: Timaeus "maintained his position as the standard authority on the history of the Greek West for nearly five centuries" (`sources/modern/ancient-writers-dates.md`). Modern summary.

## Why this matters for the game

Diodorus, our main narrator for 405 to 395, drew on both (and on Ephorus), so the history the game is built from already mixes a friend's and an enemy's account. The voices sample shows how far choice of lines and stated motives alone can move a story. The game itself shows no historian's voice yet; the biased-historian voice is cut from Phase 2 (`docs/scope.md`) and stays a Phase 4 idea.
