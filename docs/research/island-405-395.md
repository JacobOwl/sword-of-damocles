# The island and Carthage, 405 to 395 BC: the Phase 2 card

kind: history. The facts behind `data/island.json` and `data/carthage.json`. Every quote was checked against the saved texts in Jacob's Phoenician-Punic World repo (read-only); `scripts/verify-cast-quotes.mjs` checks them again. Translations: the Loeb Diodorus (Oldfather) and Rackham's Aristotle.

**Dates.** Diodorus counts by Athenian archon years, so give ranges. The peace is 405; the taking of Catana, Naxos and Leontini about 404 to 403; the war with Carthage 397 to 396.

**What is invention.** Every number in the game: walls, men, grain, lean, power, mood, stores, route lengths, the odds of every roll. The sources give armies in tens of thousands and disagree about them (below); the game's army strength points are invention.

## The cities in 405 (the peace)

- **The peace terms.** "To the Carthaginians shall belong, together with their original colonists, the Elymi and Sicani; the inhabitants of Selinus, Acragas, and Himera as well as those of Gela and Camarina may dwell in their cities, which shall be unfortified, but shall pay tribute to the Carthaginians; the inhabitants of Leontini and Messenê and the Siceli shall all live under laws of their own making, and the Syracusans shall be subject to Dionysius" (Diodorus 13.114.1). So at the start: Syracuse is yours; Leontini and Messana are free; Gela and Acragas pay tribute and have no walls; Motya and Panormus are Carthage's.
- **Catana.** A Chalcidian city, like Naxos and Leontini (14.14.1). About 403 "Arcesilaüs, the general of the Catanians, had offered to betray the city to him" (14.15.1). The game starts it free, leaning a little away from you.
- **Leontini.** First siege failed: "Dionysius, having no engines of war, gave up the siege for the time being" (14.14.4). Then the Leontines, "reflecting on the fate of the Naxians and Catanians", gave in (14.15.4). The game uses this as the rule that a sack frightens the neighbours (milestone 4).
- **Messana.** Himilcon wanted it because "it had an excellent harbour, capable of accommodating all his ships" and the straits (14.56.1). When it fell in 396, "the great mass of the common people took to flight through the surrounding mountains" (14.57.4).
- **Gela and Acragas.** In 397 Dionysius "received first the levy from Camarina, then those of Gela and Acragas" (14.47.6). The game gives tributaries a small lean toward you.
- **Motya.** "a Carthaginian colony, which they used as their chief base of operations" (14.47.4). Dionysius "began to construct moles leading to Motyê" (14.48.3); when it fell, "the plunder yielded much silver and not a little gold" (14.53.3).
- **Panormus.** "After Himilcon had put in at Panormus and disembarked his army" (14.55.4). The game makes Motya and Panormus Carthage's landing places.
- **Aetna.** The exiled horsemen "occupied Aetnê" (13.113.3); later "an exceptionally strong fortress" (14.58.2). Where it stood is uncertain; the map places it roughly on the slopes of Etna.
- **Map positions** are the sites' coordinates from Pleiades (via the Phoenician-Punic World place records), except Aetna (approximate). The coastline is Natural Earth (public domain).

## Carthage

- **The council.** Aristotle (writing about 340 to 330, so about two generations later; it may not fit 405 exactly): "The reference of some matters and not of others to the popular assembly rests with the kings in consultation with the Elders in case they agree unanimously, but failing that, these matters also lie with the people" (*Politics* 1273a). The people "have the sovereign decision, and anybody who wishes may speak against the proposals introduced" (1273a). The Boards of Five do "the election by these boards of the supreme magistracy of the Hundred" (1273a). Generals are chosen for wealth and merit; "the greatest offices of state, the kingship and the generalship, should be for sale" is his complaint (1273a). The game's blocs (the Elders, the Hundred, the people) come from this passage. What each bloc wants is invention.
- **Diodorus' word is "senate".** In 397 Dionysius' herald "delivered the letter to the senate" (14.47.2), and Carthage "dispatched members of the senate with large sums of money to recruit mercenaries in Europe" (14.47.3). The game's Hire mercenaries action is this.
- **The Libyans.** Before the war, plague "had destroyed the larger part of the inhabitants of Libya" (14.45.3). For 396 "they gathered armaments from all Libya as well as from Iberia, summoning some from their allies and in other cases hiring mercenaries" (14.54.5). The Libyan revolt after 396 is in Diodorus 14.77.1 to 14.77.3, which is **not among the saved texts**; the game's Libyan revolt rule is invention until it is saved (request in `docs/later.md`).
- **Himilcon.** The council was "lawfully according Himilcon sovereign power" (14.54.5). After the plague and defeat at Syracuse "he passed sentence of death upon himself and starved himself to death" (14.76.4). Whether he is the "Himilcar" of the peace of 405 (13.114.1) is a question of spelling in the translation; the cast card treats them as one man.
- **Magon.** Admiral in 396 (14.59.1, in the cast card); about 393/392 led an army "no less than eighty thousand in number and under the command of Magon" (14.95.1).
- **Hanno the elder and Bomilcar** are invented: Punic names, invented men, so the Elders and the Hundred each have a face.
- **Propitiating the gods.** After 396, "they voted by every means to propitiate the gods who had been sinned against" (14.77.4).

## The war of 397 to 396 (for the armies and sieges)

- **Army sizes, and the disagreement.** Dionysius at Motya: "eighty thousand infantry, well over three thousand cavalry, and a little less than two hundred warships" (14.47.7). Carthage in 396, per Ephorus: "more than three hundred thousand infantry" (14.54.5); "Timaeus, on the other hand, says that the troops transported from Libya did not exceed one hundred thousand" (14.54.6). Modern historians generally doubt the larger figures. The game shows armies as "about N thousand men" from invented strength points (one point is about 5,000 men) and does not try to match either author.
- **Plague in the siege camp.** "a plague struck the army" after the Carthaginians pillaged the temple of Demeter and Corê (14.70.4), and "the terrain was marshy" at Syracuse, where the Athenians too had died of plague (14.70.5). The game's rule: a big besieging army risks plague in summer and autumn, more at Syracuse. The odds are invention.
- **The disaster.** Carthage "gazed upon one hundred and fifty thousand dead lying in heaps and unburied because of the plague" (14.76.2). Diodorus' number, not the game's.

## Towns changing sides (milestone 4)

- **Awed by a great army.** On the march to Motya in 397, "the people of Eryx were awed by the magnitude of the force and, hating the Carthaginians as they did, came over to Dionysius" (14.48.1), and "the Sicani, fearing the great size of the army, all went over to the Syracusans, and of the rest of the cities only five remained loyal to the Carthaginians" (14.48.4). Motya did not: its people, "expecting aid from the Carthaginians, were not dismayed" (14.48.1). The game's rule: a free or tribute town near a large army may go over to it; Carthage's own towns do not.
- **Going over to Carthage.** In 396, "The Siceli, who had hated Dionysius from of old and now had an opportunity to revolt, went over in a body, with the exception of the people of Assorus, to the Carthaginians" (14.58.1). The game's rule: in war, towns that dislike you, near Carthage's army, may go over to it.
- **Fear after a sack.** After Naxos and Catana were sold into slavery (14.15.2 to 14.15.3), "The Leontines, expecting that they would receive no help and reflecting on the fate of the Naxians and Catanians, were struck with terror in fear that they would suffer the same misfortune" and gave in (14.15.4). The game's rule: a town that fears you may open its gates when your army arrives, less so if Carthage's army is near ("expecting that they would receive no help").
- All the numbers in these rules (how near, how large, the roll needed, fear fading a step a year) are invention.
