# Carthaginian and Phoenician women's names

kind: history (the names); for the name generator (thread 13, `data/names.json`). Found by the source-search thread (15), 2026-11-22. Not yet added to `data/names.json`: thread 13 owns that file and decides which to use.

## What was found

Nine women's names, each from a passage that is saved and quoted. Eight are from inscriptions, one from an ancient author. Only two are from Carthage itself before the Romans, so the pool is still thin.

Most quotes are from G. A. Cooke, *A Text-book of North-Semitic Inscriptions* (Oxford, 1903), public domain, which is already saved (OCR) in the phoenician-punic-world repo under `sources/religion/`. Cooke's transliterations are kept; his "'" stands for the letter ayin or aleph.

## Cautions

- **Dates and places vary.** "Usually after 146 BC" for Neo-Punic script is the common modern dating, not checked against a saved source. Only Am-'ashtart and Sibbolet (KAI 92) are from Carthage, and only Am-'ashtart is roughly datable (Cooke: "iii-ii cent. B. c."). The Neo-Punic stones are from later North Africa; the Cypriot ones are the Phoenician East. A name in use there was probably in use at Carthage in 405 to 395, but that is an inference.
- **Women at the tophet.** Cooke notes, on one group of Carthage votive tablets, that "in four inscrr. (273. '9. '80. '81) a woman dedicates the tablet". Their names are not given in the saved text.
- **Spelling for players.** The table keeps the scholars' spellings. A plain-English form for the game (for example "Amashtart" for Am-'ashtart, "Akhotmilkat" for Ahath-milkath) would be invention and should be marked so.
