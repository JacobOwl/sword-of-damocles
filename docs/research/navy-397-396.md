# The fleet at war, 397 to 396 BC (card for switch "fleets")

Passages opened in `phoenician-punic-world/sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt` (Loeb translation), quoted as found. Ship numbers are Diodorus' and are not used in the game (the game counts squadrons, an invention).

**Who rows.**
- Citizens and hired men: "For half of the ships of war which were prepared, the pilots, officers at the bow, and rowers were drawn from citizens, while for the rest of the vessels Dionysius hired mercenaries" (14.43.4).
- Freed slaves, in the emergency of 396: "In Syracuse Dionysius set free the slaves and manned sixty ships from their numbers" (14.58.1). That the rich families resented it is our invention.
- Citizens armed are citizens bolder. After the Syracusans won at sea without him: "they, without his presence, had won a victory over the Carthaginians, they were now puffed up with pride" (14.64.3), and "up until then they had been without arms, but now because of the war they had weapons at their command" (14.64.4).

**Orders.**
- Watch the crossing: "He ordered Leptines his admiral with one hundred and twenty ships to lie in wait for any attempt by the Carthaginians to cross to Sicily" (14.53.5).
- Escort the grain: "Dionysius and Leptines had set out with warships to escort a supply of provisions" (14.64.1).
- The sea threat holds the army home: "he ran the risk of losing the city if Magon should set out with his entire fleet against Syracuse" (14.61.2); Himilcon "dispatched two hundred ships against the city" of Messana while its men were away (14.57.1).

**The sea battle at Catana, 396.**
- Close order: Leptines was sent "under orders to engage with his ships in close order, and not to break his line" (14.59.7).
- Strike ahead: "Leptines advanced with his thirty best vessels far ahead of the rest and joined battle, in no cowardly fashion, but without prudence" (14.60.2).
- The army on the shore: "if he should suffer a reverse of some kind, the ships in distress would be able to take refuge in the camp of the land forces" (14.59.6).
- Prizes: the Phoenicians "took in tow the ships they had captured, and when they had brought them in, repaired them" (14.60.7).

**Ships caught on the beach.** Himilcon hoped "he should easily seize the vessels which were hauled up on land in the harbour" at Motya (14.50.1); at Syracuse in 396 the Syracusans found "forty ships of fifty oars, which had been drawn up on the beach" and "set fire to them" (14.73.2).

**Allied squadrons** (for switch "friendsAbroad"): Polyxenus came "bringing thirty warships from his allies, with Pharacidas the Lacedaemonian as admiral" (14.63.4).

Labels: the pattern above is history; every rule and number in `data/fleets.json` is invention.
