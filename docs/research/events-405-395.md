# Events from the sources, 405 to 395 BC

kind: history. This card backs every `kind: history` record in `data/events.json`. Each quote below was searched for and found in the saved texts in Jacob's Phoenician-Punic World repo (read-only) on 2026-11-22, with `node scripts/verify-cast-quotes.mjs ../phoenician-punic-world` (it now checks the events file too).

Texts used:
- Diodorus, Loeb translation via LacusCurtius: `sources/diodorus/english/book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt` (14.1 to 14.18), `book14.loeb.txt` (14.41 to 14.77), `book14-78-96.txt`
- Plutarch, *Dion*, Perrin: `sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`
- Polyaenus, *Stratagems* 5.2, Shepherd (1793), adapted: `sources/sicily-tyrants/polyaenus-book5.shepherd.txt`
- Pseudo-Aristotle, *Oeconomica* 2.2.20, Armstrong: `sources/sicily-tyrants/pseudo-aristotle-oeconomica-2-2.armstrong.txt`

## What is history and what is game

The source gives the situation and, usually, what Dionysius did. **Everything mechanical is invention:** when an event fires (its trigger), which cast member plays a part when the source names someone not in the cast, every other choice on offer, and every number. Each record lists these under `invented`. The choice Dionysius actually made is marked `"history": true` in the data, but the game never forces it, and some of his choices are now decided by a roll (Doricus' death, Leptines' sea battle, the Spartan's answer to Theodorus).

## How much to trust each source

- **Diodorus** writes by archon year, so every date is a range: "about 404" means 404 or 403. His speeches (Theodorus, 14.65 to 14.69) are his own composition.
- **Plutarch, *Dion*** tells the elder Dionysius' stories as background to his life of Dion, mostly with **no date**. The marriage of Philistus to Leptines' daughter (*Dion* 11) may fall after 395; the game lets it happen earlier, and says so.
- **Polyaenus** (2nd century AD) collects stratagems as anecdotes, with no dates and no sources named. Treat each as a story told about Dionysius, not a dated fact. Some may be told of other tyrants too.
- ***Oeconomica* 2** is not by Aristotle; it is a later collection of money tricks, again undated.

## Disagreements and substitutions

- **Andron's tempter.** Polyaenus names "Hermocrates, who had married his sister". Diodorus and Plutarch name Polyxenus as the sister's husband (see `cast-405-395.md`). The game casts any unfriendly member of the family or the rich families as the tempter, so it never has to pick.
- **Marsyas** (Plutarch, *Dion* 9) is not in the cast. A member of the guard takes his place.
- **The first wife's death** is not used here (see `cast-405-395.md`).

## Not saved, so not used

- **Damocles and the sword.** The story is in Cicero, *Tusculan Disputations* 5.61 to 5.62. It is not among the saved texts, and the cloud could not reach it. Two saved modern pages disagree about which Dionysius it belongs to (`sources/sicily-places/wikipedia-dionysius-i.txt` says it features him "indirectly (via his son)"; `livius-syracuse-03.txt` gives it to Dionysius II). No event is built on it until the passage is saved and read. Listed in `docs/later.md`.
- Aristotle's line on Dionysius' taxes ("in five years men had contributed the whole of their substance", *Politics* 1313b, `aristotle-politics-5.rackham.txt`) was found but not made an event; the three money tricks below cover that ground.

## The events

### The keeper's confession

Source: Polyaenus, Stratagems 5.2.4.

- Andron left in charge of fortress and treasury; Hermocrates urges him to seize them. Polyaenus 5.2.4: "he left Andron in charge of the fortress and the treasury. Hermocrates advised Andron to seize both of these" (`sources/sicily-tyrants/polyaenus-book5.shepherd.txt`)
- Executed for not reporting at once; the tempter imprisoned, then banished to please his wife. Polyaenus 5.2.4: "because he had not revealed the proposal that had been made to him, immediately upon the tyrant's return" (`sources/sicily-tyrants/polyaenus-book5.shepherd.txt`)
- Invented: trigger; who plays the tempter (Polyaenus names a Hermocrates, the sister's husband); the choices other than execution and banishment; all numbers.

### A flight by night

Source: Plutarch, Dion 21.

- Polyxenus became an enemy and fled Sicily in fear. No date. Plutarch, Dion 21: "whose husband, Polyxenus, had become his enemy. When, therefore, Polyxenus was moved by fear to run away and go into exile from Sicily" (`sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`)
- Invented: trigger (his opinion below zero); the riders and the letter; all numbers.

### Theste's answer

Source: Plutarch, Dion 21.

- Theste's answer. Plutarch, Dion 21: "it would have been well for me to be called the wife of Polyxenus the exile, rather than the sister of Dionysius the tyrant" (`sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`)
- Dionysius admired her, and so did the Syracusans. Plutarch, Dion 21: "The tyrant is said to have admired Theste for this bold speech. And the Syracusans also admired the virtue of the woman" (`sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`)
- Invented: the choices other than admiring her; all numbers.

### No bride from Rhegium

Source: Diodorus 14.44.4 to 14.44.6.

- Rhegium votes against the marriage; Dionysius turns to Locri. About 398. Diodorus 14.44.5 to 14.44.6: "the Rhegians voted not to accept the marriage connection ... Now that Dionysius had failed of this design, he dispatched his ambassadors for the same purpose to the people of the Locrians" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`)
- Invented: the choices other than Locri; all numbers.

### Two brides on one day

Source: Diodorus 14.44.6 to 14.45.1; Plutarch, Dion 3.

- Doris of Locri and the quinquereme. Diodorus 14.44.6 to 14.44.7: "sued for the hand of Doris, the daughter of Xenetus, who at that time was their most esteemed citizen" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`)
- The chariot for Aristomache. Diodorus 14.44.8: "the most notable maiden among them, Aristomachê, for whom he dispatched a chariot drawn by four white horses" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`)
- Public dinners after both marriages. Diodorus 14.45.1: "After Dionysius had taken in marriage both maidens at the same time, he gave a series of public dinners for the soldiers and the larger part of the citizens" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`)
- Plutarch: both on one day. Plutarch, Dion 3: "It is said that he married both wives on one day" (`sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`)
- Invented: marrying only one, or neither; all numbers.

### A son from Locri

Source: Plutarch, Dion 3.

- Doris bore the eldest son. Plutarch, Dion 3: "Doris had the good fortune to become a mother first, and by presenting Dionysius with his eldest son she atoned for her foreign birth" (`sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`)
- Invented: timing (four seasons after the wedding); the choices; all numbers.

### Counsel in the Island

Source: Diodorus 14.8.4 to 14.9.1.

- The council and the three counsels, about 404. Diodorus 14.8.5: "Heloris, one of his friends, or as some say, his adopted father, declared to him, "Tyranny is a fair winding-sheet"" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt`)
- Polyxenus' advice. Diodorus 14.8.5: "advised him to use his swiftest horse and ride off into the domain of the Carthaginians to the Campanians" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt`)
- Philistus' advice. Diodorus 14.8.5: "it was not fitting to dash from the tyranny on a galloping horse but to be cast out, dragged by the leg" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt`)
- The five ships and the city relaxing. Diodorus 14.9.1: "having given the tyrant permission to sail away with five ships, took matters with rather less concern" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt`)
- Invented: trigger; that it can come more than once; all numbers.

### Sixty thousand hands

Source: Diodorus 14.18.

### The arms workshops

### War, while Carthage is sick

Source: Diodorus 14.45.2 to 14.46.1.

### Theodorus speaks for liberty

Source: Diodorus 14.64.5, 14.69.4, 14.70.1 to 14.70.3.

- Theodorus rises, about 396. Diodorus 14.64.5: "Theodorus, a Syracusan, who was held in high esteem among the cavalry and was considered a man of action, made bold to speak" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`)
- His demand. Diodorus 14.69.4: "if he is willing to lay down his office of his own accord, let us allow him to leave the city with his possessions" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`)
- Pharacidas vetoes. Diodorus 14.70.2: "not to overthrow the rule of Dionysius" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`)
- Gifts and banquets after. Diodorus 14.70.3: "later he won the favour of the multitude by kindly words, honouring some of them with gifts and inviting some to general banquets" (`sources/diodorus/english/book14.loeb.txt`)
- Invented: the roll for the Spartan's answer; seizing him, the promise; all numbers.
