# Carrying the war to Libya: a what-if for 398 to 395 BC (card for switch "libyaCrossing")

**No army of Syracuse crossed to Libya in this decade** (none in the saved texts). The pattern comes from Agathocles' invasion of 310 to 307 (Diodorus book 20) and Regulus' of 256 to 255 (Polybius 1.26 to 1.34, not quoted here). Passages opened in `phoenician-punic-world/sources/diodorus/english/book20.loeb.txt` (Loeb), quoted as found. Every rule and number in `data/libya.json` is invention.

**Going, and the keeper.** "Disclosing this intention to none of his friends, he set up his brother Antander as curator of the city with an adequate garrison" (20.4.1). The crossing took six days: "After they had sailed for six days and the same number of nights ... the fleet of the Carthaginians was unexpectedly seen not far away" (20.6.1).

**The Quarries (the landing).** Agathocles disembarked "at the place called Latomiae" and built a palisade (20.6.3). Latomiae means the Quarries.

**Burning the ships.** "if the retreat to the ships was cut off, in victory alone would they have hope of safety" (20.7.5). Then the regret: the soldiers "considered the vastness of the sea" (20.8.1).

**Megalepolis and White Tunis.** He "took the city by storm; and giving it over to his soldiers for pillage" (20.8.6, Megalepolis); "setting out immediately for White Tunis, as it is called, he subdued this city" and would not garrison either: he "destroyed the cities and camped in the open" (20.8.7). On the road to Megalepolis the country "was divided into gardens and plantations of every kind" with "country houses one after another" (20.8.3). Later he fortified "a camp near Tunis" and left "an adequate garrison" there (20.17.1).

**Towns coming over.** "he prevailed on some of the cities to come over to him because of fear, others because of their hatred for the Carthaginians" (20.17.1); "Taking by storm the first, Neapolis" (20.17.1); "the people of Hadrumetum surrendered their home-land because of their fright" (20.17.5). Utica was besieged in 307 (20.54 to 20.55, not quoted here).

**The Libyans.** "he defeated in battle Aelymas, the king of the Libyans, who had deserted him" (20.18.3).

**Carthage's answers.** Arm the citizens: "they led the citizen soldiers themselves into the field, in number not less than forty thousand foot-soldiers" (20.10.5). The citizens' first generals were Hanno and Bormilcar, "men who had an inherited feud" (20.10.1); in the battle Bormilcar "withdrew with the men of the front rank" (20.12.6). Recall the army from Sicily: "the Carthaginians sent messengers into Sicily to Hamilcar, begging him to send aid as soon as possible" (20.15.1). Strike Syracuse while he is away is our invention for this decade (in 310 Hamilcar was already besieging Syracuse).

**Carthage itself.** Refugees crowded in, "there had resulted a general scarcity", "but there was no danger from the siege since the city was inaccessible because of the protection afforded by the walls" (20.59.2). Carthage fell only in 146 (general knowledge, not quoted here).

**Slipping away.** "he embarked on the transport with a few followers and secretly sailed away ... abandoned his sons, whom the soldiers among other things slew when they learned of his escape" (20.69.3).

Places named in the game: The Quarries, Megalepolis, White Tunis, Neapolis, Hadrumetum, Utica, Carthage. Answers: Recall the army, Arm the citizens, Strike Syracuse. Burn the ships.
Actions in the game (their names are invention, the deeds are in the passages above): Carry the war to Libya; Burn the ships, or beach them; March in Libya (town to town); Plunder the farmland (20.8.6); Raze the town ("destroyed the cities", 20.8.7); Sail home from Libya; Slip away alone (20.69.3).
