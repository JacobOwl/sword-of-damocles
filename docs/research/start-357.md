# Start: Dion's return (about 357 to 356 BC)

kind: history (facts) and invention (game numbers). Draft for Phase 3, not built.

Saved files used (all under /home/user/phoenician-punic-world/sources/):
- diodorus/english/book16-5-20.loeb.txt (Diodorus 16.5 to 16.20)
- plutarch/dion.perrin.txt (Plutarch, Dion; cited by chapter)
- sicily-tyrants/plato-letters-7-8.bury.txt (Plato, Letters 7 and 8)
- sicily-tyrants/aristotle-politics-5.rackham.txt (Aristotle, Politics 5)
- sicily-tyrants/polyaenus-book5.shepherd.txt (Polyaenus 5)

Rule for this page: every quote is copied from those files. Where the texts are silent I say not in the saved texts and give a proposal marked (invention).

## Dates

- Diodorus puts Dion's landing in the archonship of Agathocles at Athens (16.9.1). Dating that archonship to a BC year is not in the saved texts. Range used here: 357 to 356 BC. The year is unverified in the saved texts; check a card before building.
- Plutarch gives the voyage in days: 12 days to Pachynus, then a storm, then 5 days to Minoa. Exact dates are not in the saved texts.
- Order of events (both sources agree on the shape): landing at Minoa, march on Syracuse, entry into the city, Dionysius back by sea about a week later, a failed truce, the tyrant leaves later in the same war.
- The phrase seven days is the only fixed gap: Diodorus 16.11.3 and Plutarch, Dion 29.
- Length of the siege of Ortygia before Dionysius sails: not in the saved texts. Range for the game (invention): about one to two years, so the start covers three to six seasons of pressure before the tyrant goes.
- Later events the start should lead toward (outside this start): Nypsius's relief fleet, the sack of Achradina, Callippus's plot.

## The six power objects

1. Citadel (Ortygia, the acropolis in Plutarch, "the citadel on the Island" in Diodorus).
   - Holder at start: the tyrant's garrison. Diodorus 16.11.5: "opened the gates of the citadel on the Island". Dion walls it off: Plutarch, Dion 29: "he walled off the acropolis".
   - After Dionysius sails, command passes to his son: Plutarch, Dion 37: "the tyrant handed over the citadel to Apollocrates, his eldest son".
   - Starting strength: Diodorus 16.13.1: "left a considerable garrison in his citadels". Number of men: not in the saved texts.
2. Treasury.
   - Not in the saved texts as a single hoard. What exists: access under the old tyrant, Plutarch, Dion 4: "Dionysius ordered his treasurers to give Dion whatever he asked". The tyrant also holds revenue land: Plutarch, Dion 37: "the revenues of Gyarta, a large and rich tract in the territory of Syracuse".
   - The city is poor: Diodorus 16.17.3: "the city was short of funds". Proposal (invention): the tyrant starts rich but cut off by sea, the city starts poor, Dion's money is his private gold and silver (Plutarch, Dion 23 describes his plate).
3. Mint.
   - Not in the saved texts. No quote on coin, mint or silver issue appears in the Dion or Diodorus 16.5 to 16.20 files.
   - Proposal (invention): the mint is on the Island with the garrison, so who holds the citadel can pay in new coin. Mark it invention in the data.
4. Fleet.
   - Tyrant: Diodorus 16.9.2: "four hundred ships of war". Plutarch, Dion 14 gives the same size in the court's complaint: "abandon his four hundred triremes and his ten thousand horsemen". Diodorus 16.13.3: "being in control of the sea".
   - Dionysius took part of it away: Plutarch, Dion 26: "it chanced that he had recently sailed with eighty ships to Italy".
   - City fleet: Diodorus 16.13.3 says the Syracusans "had many ships of war" and Plutarch, Dion 35 says the citizens "derived their power from their fleet". Diodorus 16.16.3 gives "about the same number" as Philistus's sixty.
   - Whether the 400 are all fit for sea: not in the saved texts. Proposal (invention): game numbers about 80 at the Island, 60 under Philistus, and the rest rotting or abroad.
5. Generalship.
   - Held by the assembly's vote, not by birth. Diodorus 16.10.3: "The crowd as with one voice cried out that it chose Dion and his brother Megacles as generals with absolute power."
   - Plutarch, Dion 29 adds a board: "chose twenty colleagues to hold office with them".
   - Heracleides takes the fleet command: Diodorus 16.16.2: "he was chosen admiral by the Syracusans". Plutarch, Dion 33: "chose Heracleides admiral".
   - On the tyrant's side the general at sea is Philistus: Diodorus 16.16.3 "Philistus, who had been appointed general and had fitted out sixty triremes".
6. Assembly.
   - Meets in the open and decides. Plutarch, Dion 33: "they held an assembly of their own calling". Its first act is to vote Dion general (above).
   - It can also vote land: Plutarch, Dion 37: "Heracleides persuaded the Syracusans to vote this measure". It can vote away the mercenaries' pay (same chapter).
   - It is fickle: Diodorus 16.17.1: "the people, persuaded by their inopportune demagogues, refused".

## The factions

1. The tyrant's household and garrison.
   - Head: Dionysius II, away in Italy at the start. Plutarch, Dion 26: "it chanced that he had recently sailed with eighty ships to Italy". Diodorus 16.10.2 puts him instead in "the newly founded cities along the Adriatic". See Disagreements.
   - Friends left in the city: Plutarch, Dion 26: Timocrates "stood at the head of the tyrant’s friends now left in the city".
   - Informers: Plutarch, Dion 28: "tale-bearers, wicked men whom the gods hated".
   - Mercenaries: Diodorus 16.19.3: "The soldiers of the tyrant numbered more than ten thousand". Plato, 7.348 says the tyrant tried "contrary to his father’s practice, to reduce the pay of the older members of his mercenary force". Loyalty is bought, not given.
2. Dion's party and his mercenaries.
   - Size: Plutarch, Dion 22: "They numbered fewer than eight hundred". Diodorus 16.9.5: "taking along the mercenaries numbering a thousand". See Disagreements.
   - Exiles who joined: Plutarch, Dion 22: "only twenty-five took part in the expedition".
   - Marching crowd: Plutarch, Dion 27: "No fewer than five thousand men had joined him on the march". Diodorus 16.9.6 says "more than twenty thousand".
   - Mood: the crowd greets him as a god (Plutarch, Dion 29 "as if he were a god"), then turns on his manner (below).
3. Heracleides and the people.
   - Heracleides: Plutarch, Dion 32: "irresolute, fickle, and least to be relied upon as partner in an enterprise involving power and glory".
   - His appeal: Plutarch, Dion 32: "a certain natural gift of persuading and moving a populace that seeks to be courted".
   - His programme: Plutarch, Dion 37: "urging that liberty was based on equality, and slavery on the poverty of those who had naught".
   - The crowd who back him: Plutarch, Dion 48: "the mob of sailors and day-labourers".
   - The fleet is their strength: Plutarch, Dion 35: "derived their power from their fleet".
4. The rich and the men of note.
   - Plutarch, Dion 28: "the men of note and cultivation, in fresh apparel, went to meet them at the gates".
   - Plutarch, Dion 48: "The aristocracy approved of this motion" (Dion's election with full powers).
   - Plutarch, Dion 44: "the horsemen and more reputable citizens" urged Dion to hurry to the city.
   - Their stake: land. Plutarch, Dion 48: "they insisted upon the redistribution of land and houses, he opposed them". Whether the rich themselves held the land, or what share, is not in the saved texts.
   - Disarmed by the tyrant earlier: Diodorus 16.10.1: "Dionysius, being suspicious of the Syracusans, had disarmed many of them".
5. Carthaginian-held towns and Greek allies (a minor bloc). See the next two sections.

## Carthage's posture

- Peace with the tyrant: Diodorus 16.5.2: "he made peace with them". 
- Carthage holds part of the south coast. Plutarch, Dion 25: "a little town in that part of Sicily which the Carthaginians controlled". Diodorus 16.9.4: "this city was subject to the Carthaginians".
- Friendly toward Dion's landing: Plutarch, Dion 25: "Dion restored the city to Synalus, without doing it any harm", and the Carthaginian commander Synalus was "a guest-friend of Dion’s".
- Dion had links in Carthage before: Plutarch, Dion 5: "the management of the most important embassies" (the same chapter names Carthage). The tyrant later accused him over a letter: Plutarch, Dion 14: "which Dion had written to the Carthaginian officials".
- Fear of Carthage at the old tyrant's death: Plutarch, Dion 6: "the peril that threatened the realm from Carthage".
- Carthage's own plans, troop numbers, any treaty line: not in the saved texts. Proposal (invention): Carthage starts neutral and watches, ready to move if the island splits.

## The island

- Acragas, Gela, Camarina and inland Sicans and Sicels join Dion's march. Diodorus 16.9.5: "persuaded the peoples of Acragas, Gela, and some of the Sicanians and Sicels who dwelt in the interior, also the people of Camarina". Plutarch, Dion 26 adds "two hundred horsemen belonging to the Agrigentines" and "men of Gela".
- Leontini revolts: Diodorus 16.16.1: "Leontini, which had revolted from Dionysius". The Leontines and Campanians also leave the tyrant's post on Epipolae: Plutarch, Dion 27.
- Italy side: Diodorus 16.5.3: "In Apulia he founded two cities". The tyrant keeps Italian bases (Caulonia in both Plutarch and Diodorus, Locri in Diodorus 16.18.1).
- Messana, Tauromenium, Rhegium, Syracusan colonies: only Rhegium appears (Diodorus 16.16.1, as the crossing point for cavalry). The rest is not in the saved texts.
- Proposal (invention): Acragas and Gela start as swing cities, Leontini as a restless satellite, and the Italian bases as the tyrant's escape route.

## People (about 15 real, 4 invented)

Real people first. Each real person has a quote.

1. Dion (history). Brother of Aristomache; Arete's uncle and her husband. Leader of the return. Diodorus 16.6.3: "far surpassed the other Syracusans of his time". Plutarch, Dion 12: "not that he approved of a democracy, but he thought it altogether better than a tyranny in lack of a sound and healthy aristocracy".
2. Dionysius II (history). Tyrant, away at the start. Diodorus 16.5.1: "indolent and much inferior to his father". Plutarch, Dion 9: "by nature Dionysius did not belong to the worst class of tyrants".
3. Heracleides (history). Exile, admiral, leader of the people. Quotes above (Plutarch, Dion 32). Diodorus 16.17.3: "it was believed that he would never aim at tyrannical power".
4. Callippus (history). Athenian, comrade of Dion, later his killer. Plutarch, Dion 28: "on the other, Callippus the Athenian, both crowned with garlands". Plutarch, Dion 54: "altogether expecting that he would have Sicily as a reward for murdering his friend".
5. Megacles (history). Dion's brother, co-general. Diodorus 16.10.3: "chose Dion and his brother Megacles as generals with absolute power".
6. Aristomache (history). Dion's sister, widow of Dionysius I, mother of Arete. Plutarch, Dion 3: "Aristomache, daughter of Hipparinus, who was a leading man in Syracuse". Plutarch, Dion 51: "Aristomache leading Dion’s son, while Arete followed after them in tears".
7. Arete (history). Dion's wife, forced to marry Timocrates. Plutarch, Dion 21: "he gave Arete in marriage, against her will, to Timocrates, one of his friends". Plutarch, Dion 6: "Arete became the wife of Dion, her uncle".
8. Hipparinus and Nysaeus, sons of Dionysius I by Aristomache (history). Diodorus 16.6.2: "two sons Hipparinus and Nysaeus". Plutarch, Dion 6 counts four children by Aristomache, "two of whom were daughters, Sophrosyne and Arete". Note: a different Hipparinus is Dion's father (Diodorus 16.6.1 "Dion, son of Hipparinus") and Plutarch, Dion 31 gives the name Hipparinus to Dion's own son. Keep these separate in the game.
9. Philistus (history). Tyrant's admiral and historian. Plutarch, Dion 11: "for a long time was commander of the garrison that guarded the citadel". Diodorus 16.16.3 "the most faithful of their friends to the men in power".
10. Apollocrates (history). Dionysius II's eldest son, left in the citadel. Plutarch, Dion 37: "the tyrant handed over the citadel to Apollocrates, his eldest son".
11. Nypsius (history). Neapolitan commander, brought by Dionysius with food. Diodorus 16.18.1: "Nypsius the Neapolitan, a man who excelled in valour". Plutarch, Dion 41: "who brought food and money for the beleaguered garrison".
12. Timocrates (history). Dion's wife's second husband, chief of the tyrant's friends in the city. Plutarch, Dion 26: "who stood at the head of the tyrant’s friends now left in the city".
13. Theodotes (history). Dion's friend in Plutarch, Dion 12 and later Heracleides's ally. Plutarch, Dion 12: "he had been in conference with Theodotes and Heracleides concerning a subversion of the government". Plutarch, Dion 47: "Heracleides and Theodotes, however, came of their own accord and surrendered themselves to Dion".
14. Timonides the Leucadian (history). Dion's officer and letter writer. Plutarch, Dion 22: "Timonides the Leucadian". Plutarch, Dion 30: "he put Timonides in command of these".
15. Synalus (history). Carthaginian commander at Minoa. Plutarch, Dion 25: "Synalus, the Carthaginian commander, was in the place, and he was a guest-friend of Dion’s".
16. Hippo (history). Mover of land redistribution for Heracleides. Plutarch, Dion 37: "he induced Hippo, one of their leaders, to make proposals to the people for a distribution of land".
17. Sosis (history). Demagogue who faked an attack. Plutarch, Dion 34: "exchanged a stupid and drunken tyrant for a watchful and sober master".
18. Miltas (history). Thessalian seer with Dion. Plutarch, Dion 22: "Miltas the Thessalian also, who was a seer and had studied in the Academy".
19. Paralus (history). Carthaginian governor at Minoa in Diodorus, a friend of Dion. Diodorus 16.9.4: "its governor, named Paralus, who was a friend of Dion". Plutarch names Synalus for the same place. See Disagreements.

Invented (names reused from saved texts; the invented person is not the one in the text):

20. Lysis (invention). A Syracusan horseman of the old landowning class, torn between Dion and the tyrant. Reason: the sources name the horsemen as a group (Plutarch, Dion 44) but give no individual.
21. Damon (invention). A ship's captain in the city fleet, loyal to Heracleides. Reason: the fleet decides who rules and has no named captains in the saved texts.
22. Theodorus (invention). A grain contractor who can feed or starve the Island. Reason: Diodorus 16.13.3 says grain is the tyrant's weak point; no supplier is named.
23. Lycon (invention). A captain of the Island garrison who may sell the citadel. Reason: Diodorus 16.18.2 says the garrison "voted to surrender the citadel", and Plutarch, Dion 37 shows the price negotiated, so officers have a price.

## What the player starts as

- Default: Dion. He has the clearest goals and the clearest warnings. His starting numbers (invention): generalship by acclamation, about 800 to 1,000 mercenaries (see Disagreements), little money, no citadel.
- Second start: Heracleides. A seasoned officer with a fleet and a crowd. Goal: win the people and the sea. Risk: Dion's gravity beats him in the assembly and then he can win only by land redistribution.
- Third start: Timocrates, the tyrant's man in the city. Plutarch, Dion 26: "who stood at the head of the tyrant’s friends now left in the city". Goal: hold the city until Dionysius returns. The sources say he fled. Success in the game would be a different history.
- Alternative (cheaper): Dionysius II himself, in Italy, deciding when to come back. The texts give his unwillingness to rule hard: Diodorus 16.9.3 "the pusillanimity of the tyrant". Marked invention for the start state.

## Stories this start can make

1. The liberator who becomes the feared man. Plutarch, Dion 32: "they found occasion for suspecting and fearing him". Plutarch, Dion 52: "self-will was a companion of solitude". Plutarch, Dion 34: Sosis calls him "a watchful and sober master". Simulation hook: Dion's stern manner lowers the crowd's mood, and the people look for a flatterer.
2. The demagogue who wants land. Plutarch, Dion 37: "urging that liberty was based on equality, and slavery on the poverty of those who had naught". Plutarch, Dion 48: "they insisted upon the redistribution of land and houses, he opposed them". Simulation hook: land votes split rich and poor, and Dion's mercenaries lose their pay.
3. The mercenaries as a power of their own. Diodorus 16.17.3: "in excess of three thousand". Plutarch, Dion 38: "put Dion in their midst, encompassed him about". Simulation hook: unpaid soldiers can protect Dion or turn against the city.
4. The false peace and the ambush. Diodorus 16.11.5: "Dionysius, having placed them under guard, postponed the conference". Plutarch, Dion 30: "plied his mercenaries with strong wine". Simulation hook: the garrison can attack during talks.
5. The trusted friend who kills him. Plutarch, Dion 54: Callippus "was always reporting to Dion various speeches of his soldiers against him". Simulation hook: a close ally gains soldiers' trust while reporting rumours; the player (or Dion) is blind to it.

## Disagreements (readings shown, not chosen)

- Where Dionysius was. Plutarch, Dion 26: "it chanced that he had recently sailed with eighty ships to Italy". Diodorus 16.10.2: "the newly founded cities along the Adriatic". Diodorus 16.11.3 also places him "near Caulonia in Italy".
- Dion's mercenaries. Plutarch, Dion 22: "They numbered fewer than eight hundred". Diodorus 16.9.5: "taking along the mercenaries numbering a thousand".
- Crowd size at the gates. Plutarch, Dion 27: "No fewer than five thousand men had joined him on the march". Diodorus 16.10.5: "The whole number of the soldiers with Dion was not less than fifty thousand".
- The Carthaginian officer at Minoa. Plutarch, Dion 25 names Synalus. Diodorus 16.9.4 names Paralus. The two are not shown to be the same person.
- Heracleides. Diodorus 16.6.4: "Heracleides who had been appointed commandant of the garrison by the tyrant" went with Dion. Plutarch, Dion 32: "He had quarrelled with Dion in Peloponnesus".
- Heracleides's character. Diodorus 16.17.3: "it was believed that he would never aim at tyrannical power". Plutarch, Dion 32: "irresolute, fickle, and least to be relied upon".
- Dion's reputation. Diodorus 16.20.6: "honoured their benefactor as the one and only saviour of their native land". Plutarch, Dion 32: "they found occasion for suspecting and fearing him".
- The death of Philistus. Diodorus 16.16.3: "Philistus, with apprehensions of torture after his capture, slew himself". Plutarch, Dion 35: "Ephorus, it is true, says that when his ship was captured, Philistus slew himself". Plutarch gives Timonides's account that he was taken alive.
- Dion's son's name. Plutarch, Dion 31: "Timaeus, it is true, says he was called Aretaeus". Plutarch's own view: Hipparinus. Polyaenus 5: "Hipparion was the name of Dion's son".
- Who ends the truce. Diodorus 16.11.5: Dionysius "suddenly opened the gates of the citadel on the Island". Polyaenus 5: "the citizens gave themselves up to intemperate joy at the recovery of their liberty". The texts agree on the trick, differ on who was fooled.
- How the tyrant was overthrown. Aristotle, Politics 5: "Dionysius was put down by Dion , his relative, who got the people on to his side and expelled him, but was afterwards killed." Diodorus 16.9.3 gives the cause as "the pusillanimity of the tyrant" and "his subjects' hatred of him".
- Plato's picture of Dion. Plato, Letter 7.324: "he believed that the Syracusans ought to be free and dwell under the best laws". Plutarch, Dion 12 says he preferred an aristocracy and would take a democracy over a tyranny.

## Not found in the saved texts

- Exact year of the landing (only the Athenian archon is named).
- Mint, coinage, or silver issue by either the tyrant or the city.
- A figure for the treasury, or its location.
- How many men were in the Island garrison.
- Number of ships fit for sea.
- Carthage's own plans or troop numbers.
- A named list of rich Syracusan families.
- Other Sicilian cities not mentioned above (Messana, Tauromenium, Catana, Naxos).
- Any detail on the assembly's size, rules, or meeting place beyond the words market-place, theatre and assembly.
