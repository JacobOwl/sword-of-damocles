## Dates

The saved Diodorus dates the coup by Athenian and Roman officials, not by BC years. It says "Demogenes was archon in Athens" and that "Agathocles of Syracuse became tyrant of his city" (Diodorus 19.2.1). A BC year is not in the saved ancient texts.

- Coup and massacre: 317 to 316 BC (range). The 317 comes from the modern Wikipedia extract only: "In 317 BC, Agathocles used this force to launch a coup".
- The same extract puts his first election as general in 319/318 BC and Hamilcar's peace in 314/313 BC. These are modern, second-hand dates, and not in the saved ancient texts.
- Diodorus says Book 19 runs "up to the battle at Himera between Agathocles and the Carthaginians, embracing a period of seven years" (Diodorus 19.1.10). Counting seven years from the coup puts that battle at about 311 to 310. That is my arithmetic, not a stated date.
- The year of the war with Acragas and the peace that ended it is not given in the saved Diodorus (Diodorus 19.70 to 19.71). Range for the game: after the coup and before the Carthaginian war, so about 316 to 311.

Game clock proposal (invention): start in the first season after the two days of killing. The first Assembly vote and the first sword-check warning both fall in the first year.

## The six power objects

**Citadel.** Holder: Agathocles, through his soldiers. The saved texts name no citadel and no Island fortress for this date. They do say "All the gates of the city were closed" (Diodorus 19.8.1), so his men held the walls. Proposal (invention): the Island fortress is held by a garrison picked from the interior troops.

**Treasury.** Holder: Agathocles personally, as general. "He kept a careful watch over the public revenues" (Diodorus 19.9.7). The killers were told "to plunder the property of the Six Hundred and their supporters" (Diodorus 19.6.5). The size of the treasury is not in the saved texts. Proposal (invention): a public purse plus a heap of seized estates, tracked as two separate numbers.

**Mint.** Not in the saved texts. Proposal (invention): a state mint under whoever holds the generalship, so coin quality is a lever for paying soldiers.

**Fleet.** Holder: Agathocles. "he had warships constructed in addition to those already at hand" (Diodorus 19.9.7). The number of ships at this date is not in the saved texts.

**Generalship.** Holder: Agathocles, alone, with no colleagues. "he was elected general with absolute power" (Diodorus 19.9.4). He refused a board: "he would not consent as one member of a board to be held legally accountable for acts illegally committed by the others" (Diodorus 19.9.4). Before this he was "general and protector of the peace" (Diodorus 19.5.5).

**Assembly.** It still meets, but it is packed and frightened. He called it together himself (Diodorus 19.9.1). Its members "had had a share in his unholy acts" so would not give the generalship to anyone else (Diodorus 19.9.2). He promised in it "both to abolish debts and to distribute land to the poor" (Diodorus 19.9.5).

Design note. A sixth object has just been destroyed: the club of the Six Hundred, "the society of the Six Hundred, which had directed the city in the time of the oligarchy" (Diodorus 19.5.6). The game could keep it as an empty slot that someone may try to refill.

## The factions

1. **The Six Hundred and the old rich (broken).** "the chief group opposed to Agathocles was the society of the Six Hundred" (Diodorus 19.5.6). Membership: "the Syracusans who were first in reputation and in property had been enrolled in this society" (Diodorus 19.5.6). Leaders: "Peisarchus and Diocles, who were regarded as the leaders of the society of the Six Hundred" (Diodorus 19.6.4). Goal in game: survive, flee, or buy back their land.
2. **Agathocles' soldiers from the interior.** About three thousand men from Morgantina and other inland cities: "These soldiers numbered about three thousand" (Diodorus 19.6.3). They were "unceasingly hostile to the Six Hundred" (Diodorus 19.6.3). Goal: plunder and pay.
3. **The poor and indebted.** "many of those who were poor and involved in debt welcomed the revolution" (Diodorus 19.9.5). Goal: debt cancellation and land.
4. **The frightened citizens.** "Of the Syracusans who were uncorrupted, some were forced to endure in patience because of their fears" (Diodorus 19.9.5). Goal: stay alive and avoid the next purge.
5. **The exiles.** "The number of those who were driven from their native city was more than six thousand, most of whom fled to the people of Acragas" (Diodorus 19.8.2). Goal: return, with foreign help.
6. **Acragas, Gela and Messene as a bloc.** "the popular assembly of the Acragantines voted for the war, added the people of Gela and Messenê to the alliance" (Diodorus 19.70.2). Their reason, spoken by the exiles: better to fight "before the tyrant became strong" (Diodorus 19.70.1).
7. **The Corinthian mechanism (background).** Syracuse used to ask Corinth for a general. "Acestorides the Corinthian had been elected general in Syracuse" (Diodorus 19.5.1). A game faction of foreign generals could reuse this.

## Carthage's posture

Carthage is first an ally of the exiles, then a peace-maker, then a worried neighbour.

- Allied to the exiles: "the Carthaginians became allies of the exiles with Sostratus" (Diodorus 19.4.3).
- Peace: the Syracusans "made peace with the Carthaginians" (Diodorus 19.5.4).
- Watching Agathocles: envoys came to Messene and "censured Agathocles for what he had done on the ground that he had violated the treaty" (Diodorus 19.65.5).
- Mediator: "Hamilcar the Carthaginian acting as mediator in making the treaty" (Diodorus 19.71.6). The terms: "Heraclea, Selinus, and Himera were to be subject to the Carthaginians" ... "all the others were to be autonomous under the hegemony of Syracuse" (Diodorus 19.71.7).
- Home politics: "the Carthaginians, who had censured Hamilcar for the terms of peace, would shortly wage war against him" (Diodorus 19.72.2).
- Justin tells a different and darker story of Hamilcar (see Disagreements).

Game use (invention): Carthage has a general in Sicily with a patron faction at home and an enemy faction at home. A peace he signs can later be used to attack him.

## The island

Who holds what, with the saved evidence. Anything not listed is not in the saved texts for this date.

- Syracuse: Agathocles (Diodorus 19.9.4).
- Cities of the interior: "He also gained control of most of the regions and cities of the interior" (Diodorus 19.9.7). Morgantina and neighbours supply his soldiers (Diodorus 19.6.2).
- Erbita: "some rebels were assembling an army in the interior near Erbita" (Diodorus 19.6.1). Agathocles used the news to raise troops. Whether any rebels really existed is not said.
- Gela: Agathocles' men and Sostratus' men fought at Gela (Diodorus 19.4.4). Later it joins Acragas in the league (Diodorus 19.70.2).
- Acragas: the refuge of the exiles and head of the league against him (Diodorus 19.70.1 and 19.70.2).
- Messene: Agathocles "was holding a fort of the Messenians" (Diodorus 19.65.1), then tried and failed to take the city. "many of the exiles from Syracuse had taken refuge in the city" (Diodorus 19.65.4).
- Mylae: a fort that "surrendered by capitulation" (Diodorus 19.65.3).
- Abacaenon: "an allied city", where he put to death "those who appeared to be hostile to him, being more than forty in number" (Diodorus 19.65.6).
- Carthaginian zone: Heraclea, Selinus and Himera (Diodorus 19.71.7). Therma was "a city that had been brought under the rule of the Carthaginians" (Diodorus 19.2.2).
- After the treaty he "began unhampered to subject the cities and strongholds to himself" (Diodorus 19.72.1).

## People (16)

History people first. Quotes are from the saved texts; role lines are my summary.

1. **Agathocles** (history). Potter's son, officer, tyrant of Syracuse. "a man who had the lowest beginnings" (Diodorus 19.1.6). "he was elected general with absolute power" (Diodorus 19.9.4).
2. **Carcinus of Rhegium** (history). Agathocles' father, an exile, made a Syracusan citizen under Timoleon's grant. "Carcinus was enrolled as a citizen together with Agathocles" (Diodorus 19.2.8).
3. **Damas** (history). Rich Syracusan who promoted and enriched Agathocles. "Damas had been elected general against Acragas" (Diodorus 19.3.1).
4. **Heracleides** (history). Co-commander with Sostratus; a plotter. "Heracleides and Sostratus, men who had spent the greater part of their lives in plots, murders, and great impieties" (Diodorus 19.3.3).
5. **Sostratus** (history). Led the cabal, then the exiles, with Carthaginian allies. "Sostratus with a large force in battle array appeared suddenly" (Diodorus 19.4.4). Whether he is the same man as "Sosistratus, who was the most distinguished of the exiles" (Diodorus 19.71.4) is not settled in the saved texts.
6. **Acestorides the Corinthian** (history). Foreign general who tried to have Agathocles killed. "ordered him to leave the city and sent out men to kill him on the road during the night" (Diodorus 19.5.1).
7. **Peisarchus** (history). Leader of the Six Hundred, arrested in the trap. "Peisarchus and Diocles, who were regarded as the leaders of the society of the Six Hundred" (Diodorus 19.6.4).
8. **Diocles** (history). The other leader of the Six Hundred; same line (Diodorus 19.6.4).
9. **Deinocrates** (history). Spared by Agathocles, later general of the exiles. "he let Deinocrates go because of their former friendship" (Diodorus 19.8.6).
10. **Antandrus** (history). Agathocles' brother and an army general. "Antandrus, the brother of Agathocles, was one of the generals of this army" (Diodorus 19.3.3).
11. **Hamilcar** (history). Carthaginian general in Sicily, mediator, blamed at home. "Hamilcar the Carthaginian acting as mediator in making the treaty" (Diodorus 19.71.6).
12. **Acrotatus** (history). Spartan prince hired by Acragas; he killed a leading exile and was driven out. "he finally invited to dinner Sosistratus" (Diodorus 19.71.4). "he took flight by night and sailed secretly to Laconia" (Diodorus 19.71.5).
13. **Megacles of Messene** (history, date not given). Opposed Agathocles. "He aroused many of the Sicilians against Agathocles, and offered a reward to anyone who assassinated him" (Polyaenus 5.15). Polyaenus gives no date, so he may not belong at 317.
14. **Timoleon** (history, dead background). Source of the citizenship grant. Diodorus says the grant was made "on all who wished" (Diodorus 19.2.8).
15. **A Morgantina captain, Philonides** (invention). Led a company of inland soldiers within the three thousand. Reason: the soldiers are the main power base but none is named. The name is borrowed from a different real man in Diodorus 19.104.2 and used here only as a name.
16. **A Six Hundred survivor, Demophilus** (invention). A noble who was not in the room that morning and must now choose between exile and silence. Reason: the texts give "more than four thousand persons" killed but name only a few. The name is borrowed from a different real man in Diodorus 19.104.2.

Spare invention slots, names borrowed from the texts: a debtor farmer, Paron; a priest or priestess at the shrine of Demeter where the oath was sworn (Diodorus 19.5.4); a Carthaginian agent, Menedemus; a restless chiliarch, Stilpo. Use only if the roster needs them.

## What the player starts as

**Main start (invention): a chiliarch of the interior troops.** A commander of a thousand, as Agathocles himself once was ("one of his chiliarchs died", Diodorus 19.3.1). The player owes the general, shares the plunder, and chooses: stay loyal, take a cut, or sell out. The sword check warns about the general's habit of killing those who followed him, as at Abacaenon (Diodorus 19.65.6).

**Alternate start A (invention): an exile at Acragas.** One of the six thousand. Lobby the Acragantine assembly, back the Spartan prince, or take Carthaginian money. The built-in tension is that the exile leaders murder each other (Diodorus 19.71.4).

**Alternate start B (invention): a Six Hundred survivor in Syracuse.** A noble who was away on the morning. Keep your land by cheering in the Assembly, or plot. The built-in tension: the next purge is near, since the texts say "the whole city filled with outrage, slaughter" (Diodorus 19.7.2 gives the scene).

## The mercenary market at this date

What the saved texts say. Several lines belong to later years and are marked so. This feeds the mercenary-market design.

- **Origins.** Tarentum enrolled Agathocles "in the ranks of the mercenaries" (Diodorus 19.4.1). Exiles from Croton went out "taking with them three hundred mercenaries" (Diodorus 19.10.4). In the African war (later) his line had "three thousand Greek mercenaries" and "three thousand Samnites, Etruscans, and Celts" (Diodorus 20.11.1).
- **Size, soon after this start.** After the treaty: "a mercenary force comprising ten thousand foot soldiers and thirty-five hundred horse" (Diodorus 19.72.2). This is the best number for a first-years budget check.
- **Pay and arrears (later, African war).** Justin: his army mutinied because "the discharge of their arrears of pay had been deferred" (Justin 22.8.4). Diodorus: soldiers "kept demanding the pay that was due them" (Diodorus 20.33.8). Carthage tried to buy them with "greater pay and noteworthy bonuses" (Diodorus 20.34.1). A besieged city jeered, "Potter and furnace-man, when will you pay your soldiers?" and he answered "when I have taken this city" (Diodorus 20.63.5).
- **How pay was found.** In Africa-war year: he took orphans' money, borrowed from merchants and "took some of the dedications in the temples" (Diodorus 20.4.5), and killed rich men and confiscated property (Diodorus 20.4.7). In 317 itself, the source of cash was the seized estates of the Six Hundred (Diodorus 19.6.5).
- **Land and freedom.** The only land promise at the start is to the poor: "to distribute land to the poor" (Diodorus 19.9.5). Citizenship in earlier days: Timoleon gave it "on all who wished" (Diodorus 19.2.8). Slaves (later): "he freed those of their slaves who were fit for military service" (Diodorus 20.4.8).
- **Mercenaries as the strong arm.** "sending after them some of his mercenaries, he killed the men themselves" (Diodorus 20.4.7, later). Later still, when Ligurians and Etruscans "mutinously demanded their pay" he "put them all to death" (Diodorus 21.3.1).
- **Seizing cities.** Messene: he took a fort for thirty talents and then tried to take the city (Diodorus 19.65.1 and 19.65.2). Garrisons: "making them secure with garrisons and exacting money from them" (Diodorus 20.77.1, after 311).
- **A foreign taste.** Polybius says Campanian mercenaries of Agathocles later seized Messene (Polybius 1.7 is in the 289 dossier).

## Stories this start can make

1. **The promise of land and no debt.** Agathocles promised the Assembly "both to abolish debts and to distribute land to the poor" (Diodorus 19.9.5). The game should track the promise as an obligation. If he does not deliver, the poor cool toward him. Diodorus also notes the turn from terror to charm: "he made an end of further slaughter and punishment" (Diodorus 19.9.6). A mood change, not a script.
2. **The oath at the shrine.** Diodorus 19.5.4 says he swore at the shrine of Demeter that "he would undertake nothing against the democracy". The next lines have him taking the generalship, and the coup follows. In the game, an oath is a visible object, and breaking it should cost something with the gods and the exiles.
3. **The invited foreign saviour.** Acragas sent for "a general capable of taking charge of affairs" (Diodorus 19.70.2). The man who came proved "bloodthirsty and more cruel than the tyrants" (Diodorus 19.71.2 gives "being bloodthirsty and more cruel than the tyrants"). The sim can produce this by itself: a city hires a general, then fears him.
4. **The mediator who gets blamed.** A Carthaginian peace gives Syracuse the lead; the signing general is attacked at home: "the Carthaginians, who had censured Hamilcar for the terms of peace" (Diodorus 19.72.2). Carthage's own factions can punish their general.
5. **A fort bought, then a city attacked.** "he not only failed to keep his promise to those who had put faith in him" (Diodorus 19.65.2). Carthage then forces him to give the fort back (Diodorus 19.65.5). Broken trust followed by outside pressure is a good sword-check warning pattern.

## Disagreements (shown, not chosen)

**How the coup went.**
- Diodorus: the Six Hundred leaders were called in as if for advice, arrested, and the soldiers were told "to kill the guilty persons" (Diodorus 19.6.5). "more than four thousand persons were slain on that day" (Diodorus 19.8.1). The Assembly then made him general (Diodorus 19.9.4).
- Justin: Agathocles first got peace and Syracusan office with Carthaginian help: "he was also appointed praetor at Syracuse" (Justin 22.2.7). Then, "having received from Hamilcar five thousand African troops, he put to death the most powerful of the leading citizens" (Justin 22.2.9). The people were called to the theatre and the senate to the Gymnasium (Justin 22.2.10), and the soldiers "caused the senate to be massacred" (Justin 22.2.11).
- Polyaenus (a stratagem collection): Agathocles takes off his robe and sword and says he is a private citizen, and then, "having put many of the citizens to death, and driven more than five thousand into exile" he took the rule (Polyaenus 5.3.7). A separate trick, with the names Diocles, Tisarchus and Anthropinus, is set at the Timoleonteium (Polyaenus 5.3.8). Diodorus has "Peisarchus and Diocles" there.

**Whose oath.** Diodorus: an oath to the citizens at the shrine of Demeter (Diodorus 19.5.4). Justin: an oath to Hamilcar, "he would be faithful to the Carthaginians" (Justin 22.2.8).

**Friend of the exiles or friend of the tyrant.** Diodorus 19.4.3 has Carthage allied with the exiles. Justin has Hamilcar making an alliance with Agathocles out of hope and "dread of his power" (Justin 22.2.6), and has Carthaginian allies calling Hamilcar "the latter as a traitor" (Justin 22.3.3). The saved Diodorus instead has his own city turn on him: "the Carthaginians, who had censured Hamilcar for the terms of peace" (Diodorus 19.72.2).

**Character and the source problem.**
- Justin: "Twice he attempted to make himself sovereign of Syracuse; and twice he was driven into exile" (Justin 22.1.16).
- Diodorus: "a man who had the lowest beginnings" (Diodorus 19.1.6) and cruel beyond other tyrants: "No one of the tyrants before him brought any such achievements to completion" (Diodorus 19.1.8). Diodorus also warns against Timaeus: "the greater part of his history of Agathocles consists of lying propaganda against the ruler because of personal enmity" (Diodorus 21.17.1), and against Callias, who sang "dishonest praises of his paymaster" (Diodorus 21.17.4).
- Polybius on Timaeus: "For my part I cannot feel satisfied with his abuse of Agathocles either, even admitting him to have been the worst of men" (Polybius 12.15). He adds that "Agathocles must have had fine natural qualities" (Polybius 12.15).
- Beginnings. Diodorus: Carcinus of Rhegium was his father and the family became citizens under Timoleon (Diodorus 19.2.7 and 19.2.8). Justin: he was a potter's son who was "received as a citizen" (Justin 22.1.2 and 22.1.6). Polybius, reporting Timaeus' claim: he "came as a runaway slave to Syracuse" (Polybius 12.15). All are given; the game need not choose.

**Modern note (Wikipedia, second-hand).** It gives the date 317 and the title "General and Guardian of the Peace". Diodorus says "general and protector of the peace" (Diodorus 19.5.5). Same office, different English.

## Not found in the saved texts

- The BC year of the coup, of the Acragas war and of the treaty. Only the archon Demogenes is named.
- Any named citadel, treasury building or mint, or its output, or where the Assembly sat. (Place names found: the Timoleontium, the theatre, the Gymnasium and a shrine of Demeter.)
- Number of citizens, number of slaves, size of the fleet at 317, size of the treasury.
- What the Six Hundred did before 317 beyond directing the city "in the time of the oligarchy".
- Where the Carthaginian army stood in Sicily at the time.
- Whether the rebels at Erbita were real.
- Anything about coin designs for this date.
- I used nothing from memory.
