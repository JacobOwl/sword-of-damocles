# Exile and return (switch "Fallen, not finished", bet B7)

What the sources say about Syracusan exiles who tried to come home, and what the game takes from them. Every quote below is from the Loeb translation of Diodorus (the saved files credit the Loeb series, Oldfather, Sherman, Welles and Geer, via LacusCurtius) as saved in the Phoenician-Punic World project (`sources/diodorus/english/book13-gaps.txt`, `book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt`, `book14.loeb.txt`, `book16-5-20.loeb.txt`), searched, not read whole. The rules and numbers of the switch are all invention; the sources give the shape of the story, not odds.

## 1. Hermocrates: an exile who built a force and failed at the gate (408 BC)

Dionysius was one of his supporters wounded in the fighting (13.75.9, below) and later married his daughter (13.96.3: he "married the daughter of Hermocrates"; saved file `book13.loeb.txt`). Hermocrates is the great model of a failed return.

- Banished: "he was overpowered by his political opponents and, upon being condemned to exile, he handed over the fleet" (13.63.1).
- A patron's silver and hired men: "he accepted from him [Pharnabazus, the Persian satrap] a great sum of money with which, after he had arrived at Messenê, he had five triremes built and hired a thousand soldiers" (13.63.2). He added "above a thousand of the Himeraeans who had been driven from their home" (13.63.3).
- Raiding Carthage's lands to win the Greeks: "Making Selinus his base he first laid waste the territory of the inhabitants of Motyê ... After this he ravaged the territory of the people of Panormus and acquired countless booty" (13.63.4); "he won the commendation of the Sicilian Greeks. And among other things the majority of the Syracusans also repented of their treatment of him" (13.63.5).
- A gesture over the heads of his enemies: he gathered the bones of the dead of Himera and sent them to Syracuse "in order that Diocles, who opposed his return ... should fall out with the masses" (13.75.4). It split the city: "civil discord arose among the masses" and "Diocles was exiled; but even so they did not receive Hermocrates back, since they were wary of the daring of the man" (13.75.5).
- Friends inside send for him: "some time later, when his friends sent for him, he set out with three thousand soldiers ... he arrived at night at the place agreed upon" (13.75.6); "Hermocrates with a small number of them came to the gate on Achradinê, and when he found that some of his friends had already occupied the region, he waited to pick up the late-comers" (13.75.7).
- The failure: "when the Syracusans heard what had happened, they gathered in the market-place under arms, and here, since they appeared accompanied by a great multitude, they slew both Hermocrates and most of his supporters" (13.75.8).
- Reported dead: "some of them who had been severely wounded were reported by their relatives as having died, in order that they might not be given over to the wrath of the multitude. Among their number was Dionysius, who later became tyrant of the Syracusans" (13.75.9).

**Used in the game:** the patron "a Persian satrap" (13.63.2); hired men and building a force at Messana; the action "Raid Carthage's lands" and the citizens' praise for it (13.63.4 to 13.63.5); the event "The unburied dead" (13.75.2 to 13.75.5); the event "Your friends send for you" and the +3 for a held gate (13.75.6 to 13.75.7); the death band of "Strike for Syracuse" (13.75.8); the event "Reported dead" (13.75.9).

## 2. The exiles at Aetna, and the revolt of 404 BC

- The exiled cavalry held a fort: "the cavalry, who had been banished at the beginning of the tyranny, occupied this outpost" (14.7.7). Aetna was "an exceptionally strong fortress" (14.58.2).
- The revolt against Dionysius set a price on his head: "The revolters also proclaimed a large reward to any who would slay the tyrant" (14.8.3).
- Flight was argued over at his council: Heloris said "Tyranny is a fair winding-sheet"; "Polyxenus, his brother-in‑law, advised him to use his swiftest horse and ride off into the domain of the Carthaginians to the Campanians, whom Himilcon had left behind to guard the districts of Sicily" (14.8.5). He stayed, and "secretly dispatched messengers to the Campanians and promised them any price they should ask" (14.8.6). They came, "twelve hundred cavalry" (14.9.2), and "three hundred mercenaries had also landed to aid the tyrant, so that his hopes revived" (14.9.3).
- Terms offered to exiles: after his victory Dionysius "dispatched ambassadors to Aetnê, asking the exiles to accept terms and return to their native land, and giving his pledged word that he would not bear enmity against them" (14.9.6). "Now certain of them, who had left behind children and wives, felt compelled to accept the offer; but the rest replied ... that he deserved the same favour, and they prayed to the gods that they might, the sooner the better, see him obtain it" (14.9.7). "These men accordingly, who would by no means put any trust in the tyrant, remained in Aetnê, watching for an opportunity against him" (14.9.8).
- Campanians at Entella in Carthage's country: "the Campanians dwelling in Entella found no fault with the Carthaginians" (14.61.5).
- Sparta and tyrants: Sparta sent Aristus to Syracuse "ostensibly pretending that they would overthrow the government, but in truth with intent to increase the power of the tyranny" (14.10.2).

**Used in the game:** the refuge "Entella, among the Campanians" (14.8.5, 14.61.5) and Aetna (14.7.7, 14.58.2); the switch "Begin fallen", where the revolt of 404 succeeds (in the sources it failed); the ruler's act "Hunt the exile" (14.8.3); the event "Terms from the city" with its two answers and followers with families going home (14.9.6 to 14.9.8); the patron "Sparta" (14.10.2).

## 3. Dion: an exile who came home (357 BC)

Outside the game's decade, but the best-told return in Diodorus.

- Escape: Dion "escaped from Sicily to the Peloponnese" (16.6.4); at Corinth "he himself began to gather mercenary troops and to collect suits of armour" (16.6.5).
- Small means: "putting ashore with two merchantmen, he could actually have overcome the despot" (16.9.2); Diodorus gives as causes "the willing support of those who were to be liberated, but still more important ... the pusillanimity of the tyrant and his subjects' hatred of him" (16.9.3).
- A friendly governor in Carthage's land: at Minoa "this city was subject to the Carthaginians, and its governor, named Paralus, who was a friend of Dion, received him enthusiastically" (16.9.4).
- Arms for the unarmed: "Dion, having unloaded from the merchantman five thousand suits of armour" (16.9.5); "there came to meet him a host of men without arms both from the countryside and from the city; for Dionysius, being suspicious of the Syracusans, had disarmed many of them" (16.10.1); "Dion distributed the five thousand suits of armour to such of the Syracusans as were unarmed" (16.10.3).
- The ruler away: "About this time the tyrant was sojourning in the newly founded cities along the Adriatic with large forces" (16.10.2), and "no one daring to come out against him" Dion "encamped in the market-place" (16.10.4).

**Used in the game:** the action "Buy arms for the citizens" (16.9.5, 16.10.1 to 16.10.3); the +2 when the ruler is away in the field (16.10.2); the rule that factions who long for you and hate the ruler rise for you (16.9.3).

## What is invention

All of it as rules: the refuges' safety and prices, the ruler's priority list (hunt, purge, court, take the field, offer terms, tighten his grip, feed the people), the hunt's three warnings and its strike, every modifier on "Strike for Syracuse" and its three bands (4 or more you take the city; 2 or 3 you are thrown back; 1 or less you die), followers wavering after a year without hope, upkeep for hired men, the Carthaginian merchant, the captain who offers his company, and the patrons' prices. Carthage's council as a patron is invention built on Polyxenus' advice (14.8.5).

## Disagreements and limits

- Diodorus is the only source opened here. Plutarch's *Dion* tells Dion's return at length and differs in numbers; it is not used.
- The number of Hermocrates' men differs within Diodorus: "three thousand soldiers" set out, but he reached the gate "with a small number of them" (13.75.6 to 13.75.7). The game does not count men in thousands.
