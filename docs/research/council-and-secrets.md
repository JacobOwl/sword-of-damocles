# The council and the secrets (rule switches "Your council argues" and "Secrets and leverage")

Both switches are rules, and rules are invention. This card holds the passages behind the few things in them that come from the sources: the war council that is the model for the council, and the five start secrets tagged `history` in `data/secrets.json`. Every quote below was found in the saved texts of the Phoenician-Punic World repo by `node scripts/verify-cast-quotes.mjs ../phoenician-punic-world`.

## A council that argues: the night of about 404 BC

Diodorus 14.8.5, during the siege of Syracuse (his year of about 404; a range, 404 or 403): "Now Heloris, one of his friends, or as some say, his adopted father, declared to him, "Tyranny is a fair winding-sheet"; but Polyxenus, his brother-in-law, advised him to use his swiftest horse and ride off into the domain of the Carthaginians to the Campanians ... Philistus, however, who composed his history after these events, declared in opposition to Polyxenus that it was not fitting to dash from the tyranny on a galloping horse but to be cast out, dragged by the leg." (`sources/diodorus/english/book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt`)

Three men, three counsels, each from his own place: the old friend, the frightened kinsman, the rich backer. That is the shape of the switch's seats (the kinsman, the old friend, the purse, and the sword for the soldiers, who are not in this scene). Which man sits in which seat at the start is the game's.

- **Disagreement.** Diodorus tells the story again at 20.78.2-3 and there gives the "dragged by the leg" line not to Philistus but to "his brother-in-law, Megacles" (`sources/diodorus/english/book20.loeb.txt`). Heloris is "the eldest of his friends" there. The texts do not settle who said it.

## Dionysius and plots, as the sources tell it

- Polyaenus 5.2.3, no date: "Dionysius always guarded against conspiracies with great care", and he spread word that a foreigner had sold him "an infallible secret for revealing conspiracies". Polyaenus is late (2nd century AD) and anecdotal; a story, not a fact. It is the flavour of the switch, not a rule. (`sources/sicily-tyrants/polyaenus-book5.shepherd.txt`)
- Plutarch, *Dion* 9: "the elder Dionysius was so distrustful and suspicious towards every body". (`sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`)

## The start secrets tagged history

Each is a deed the sources tell. That the deed has already happened by 405 BC and is still hidden is the game's choice, said on each record.

- **Philistus and the marriage into your house** (and **Leptines gives away his daughter**). Plutarch, *Dion* 11: Leptines, "who had two daughters ... gave one of them to Philistus without so much as telling Dionysius, the tyrant was wroth, put the wife of Leptines into fetters and prison, and banished Philistus from Sicily." Undated. (`sources/plutarch/dion.perrin.txt`)
- **Philistus and the story about your mother.** Plutarch, *Dion* 11: "There was a story, too, that he was very intimate with the mother of the elder Dionysius, and that the tyrant was not wholly ignorant of the fact." Plutarch calls it a story; so does the game's text ("only a story, and it may be untrue").
- **Polyxenus and the fast horse.** Plutarch, *Dion* 21: "Polyxenus, had become his enemy. When, therefore, Polyxenus was moved by fear to run away and go into exile from Sicily". Diodorus 14.8.5: he advised Dionysius "to use his swiftest horse and ride off". The flight is undated; in the game, exposing the secret makes him flee.
- **Leontini and the night alarm that won your guard.** Diodorus 13.95.4 (406/405 BC): "he pretended that he was the object of a plot and had his personal servants raise a tumult and uproar"; 13.95.5, the people then gave him "a guard of six hundred soldiers". (`sources/diodorus/english/book13-1-42-91-96.loeb.txt`) Diodorus tells it as a known trick; who in Syracuse knew it in 405 is the game's. It is your one secret at the start.

## Invention, said plainly

The other start secrets (Hipparinus' list, Theodorus and the exiles' riders, Aristotle's share of the pay, the captain's grain, Nicoteles' letters to Corinth, Damon's debt, Heloris' boast, Thearides' debts, Doricus and the dead soldier), every secret made in play, and every secret of the invented townsmen and elders are invention. So are the weights (small or deadly), the rest after a use, and all the council's lines.
