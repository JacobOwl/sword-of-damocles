# Dionysius I, 406 to 403 BC: the Phase 1 starting position

kind: history. These are the facts the 2026-11-22 paper test used. The quotes were checked against the saved texts in Jacob's Phoenician-Punic World repo (read-only):

- `sources/sicily-tyrants/aristotle-politics-5.rackham.txt`

The translations are the Loeb Diodorus (Oldfather and others, via LacusCurtius), Perrin's Plutarch and Rackham's Aristotle.

**Dates.** Diodorus counts by Athenian archon years, so give each event a range rather than one year. The rise is in 406/405, the peace in 405, and the Ortygia works, the revolt and the conquests of Catana, Naxos and Leontini in about 404 to 403.

## The rise (406/405)

- **The assembly speech.** Dionysius attacks the generals in the assembly. The magistrates fine him, and "Philistus, who later composed his History, a man of great wealth, paid the fine" (Diodorus 13.91.4).
- **Supreme power.** Members of the assembly cried out "to appoint him general with supreme power" (13.94.5). He gets a bodyguard of "six hundred soldiers" at Leontini (13.95.5).
- **The marriages.** He "married the daughter of Hermocrates" and "gave his sister in marriage to Polyxenus, the brother of Hermocrates' wife" (13.96.3).
- **The executions.** He "had his most influential opponents, Daphnaeus and Demarchus, put to death" (13.96.3).
- **Where he lived.** "In Syracuse ... he took up his quarters in the naval station" (13.96.2). The Ortygia citadel isn't built yet.
- **His origins.** He went "from a scribe and ordinary private citizen" to tyrant (13.96.4).
- **Hipparinus.** Aristotle: "Hipparinus put forward Dionysius at Syracuse" (*Politics* 1306a). Plutarch calls him "a leading man in Syracuse, and had been a colleague of Dionysius when he was first chosen general with full powers" (*Dion* 3).

## The cavalry revolt and the peace (405)

- **The revolt.** After the retreat from Gela, the cavalry "plundered the house of Dionysius ... and seizing his wife left her so ill-used" (13.112.4).
- **The counterstroke.** Dionysius burns the gate of Achradina. The cavalry who resist in the market-place "were surrounded by the mercenaries and shot down to a man" (13.113.2). The rest "fled from the city and occupied Aetnê" (13.113.3).
- **The peace with Carthage.** The terms included that the inhabitants of "Selinus, Acragas, and Himera as well as those of Gela and Camarina" pay tribute to Carthage. Leontini, Messene and the Siceli "shall all live under laws of their own making, and the Syracusans shall be subject to Dionysius" (13.114.1).
- **The plague.** The Carthaginians sailed home "having lost more than half their soldiers from the plague" (13.114.2).

## His first wife: the sources disagree

- Diodorus says she was "slain at the time the cavalry revolted" (14.44.5).
- Plutarch says she was "outrageously abused ... and in consequence put an end to her own life" (*Dion* 3).

The game should show both versions and not choose between them.

## Building the tyranny (about 404 to 403)

- **Ortygia.** He walled off the Island and "constructed on the Island at great expense a fortified acropolis" (14.7.2 to 14.7.3).
- **Houses on the Island.** He gave the houses there "to his friends and the mercenaries" (14.7.5).
- **The mutiny.** At Herbessus the citizen soldiers kill their commander, "whose name was Doricus", and send for the cavalry from Aetna (14.7.6 to 14.7.7).
- **The war council.** During the siege, Heloris, "one of his friends, or as some say, his adopted father", says "Tyranny is a fair winding-sheet". Polyxenus advises flight. Philistus says a tyrant should leave only "dragged by the leg" (14.8.5).
- **The Campanians.** Hired Campanian horsemen, "twelve hundred cavalry", break the siege (14.9.2 to 14.9.3).
- **Terms for the exiles.** Afterwards he offers the exiles at Aetna terms (14.9.6).
- **Sparta's envoy.** Sparta's agent Aristus "slew Nicoteles the Corinthian, a leader of the Syracusans" (14.10.3).
- **Disarming the citizens.** Dionysius "carried off the arms of them all" (14.10.4).
- **Aetna falls.** He takes the fortress at Aetna, "the exiles there being no match for an army of such size" (14.14.2).
- **Catana and Naxos.** Catana is betrayed by its general Arcesilaus (14.15.1). The people of Naxos and Catana are sold into slavery (14.15.2 to 14.15.3).
- **Leontini.** The Leontines are ordered to "hand over their city and enjoy citizenship in Syracuse" (14.15.4).

## His family

- **Leptines.** He is admiral by about 397 (14.48.4). Later, Dionysius "relieved Leptines of his command and appointed Thearides, his other brother, commander of the fleet" (14.102.3).
- **Two wives on one day.** He later "married two wives at once", Doris of Locri and "Aristomache, daughter of Hipparinus", and "married both wives on one day" (Plutarch, *Dion* 3).
