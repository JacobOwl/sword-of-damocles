**Caveats.** Diodorus dates by archon year, so every date is a range. His army sizes are often inflated (300,000 at Syracuse, 14.62.3, is "as some have reported"). His speeches (Theodorus 14.65 to 14.69; Nicolaus and Gylippus 13.20 to 13.32) are his own composition, and Thucydides says the same of his. Labels: **history** (quoted), **plausible** (fits the sources, reason given), **invention**.

**Corrections to our own notes.** Doricus was not a separate mutiny: soldiers killed him at Herbessus and that started the 404 revolt (14.7.6-7). "Aristotle the Spartan", arrested after the 396 victory (14.78.1), is not Aretes (14.70.3) nor Aristus (14.10). Catana is told in the same year as the 396 siege: date it 397 to 396.

## Battles

**Gela, 405** (13.109 to 13.111). Three columns, fleet and horse. The cavalry was to join "if they should see their comrades winning" (13.109.5). The fleet arrived; the rest did not: "the Sicilian Greeks... came too late and the mercenaries with Dionysius encountered difficulties in making their way through the streets" (13.110.4). Then a council of friends, "When they all said that his position was unfavourable" (13.111.1); a truce to bury the dead, and 2,000 light troops left "to keep fires burning... and to make an uproar" while he slipped away (13.111.2). Scene stages (history): plan; a timing check per column; a council, fight or slip away; the evacuation.

**Catana, sea, 397 to 396** (14.59 to 14.61). Orders to fight "in close order, and not to break his line" (14.59.7). Leptines "advanced with his thirty best vessels far ahead of the rest... in no cowardly fashion, but without prudence" (14.60.2), and "the defeat suffered by the admiral raised the spirits of the Phoenicians" (14.60.4). The killing came after, among the swimmers (14.60.5-6). Then a council: fight now or go home, and "The majority of the Sicilian Greeks... deserted" (14.61.1-3). Stages (history): the admiral's discipline; the pursuit; fight or go home, with allies leaving if you go home.

**Motya, 397** (14.48 to 14.53). The mole; the Punic fleet's surprise, and ships "hauled... over the land" (14.50.3-4); fighting from towers; the recall sounded "always toward evening", then Archylus' picked men with ladders "when night had fallen" (14.52.5-6); the sack, after which heralds told the Motyans "to take refuge in the temples" (14.53.2); Archylus paid "one hundred minas", Greek turncoats crucified (14.53.4). Stages (history): build a habit, then break it at night; the sack (restrain, sanctuary, plunder).

**Syracuse, 396** (14.62 to 14.75). The night march, "profiting by a moonless night... passing by the temple of Cyanê", the ships at daybreak (14.72.1). The cavalry told to "flee and leave the mercenaries in the lurch", so the men he distrusted died (14.72.2-3). The forts, the fleet, fire among the ships (14.72.4 to 14.73.4). After: Carthage's secret offer of "the three hundred talents which they had there in their camp" (14.75.1), taken so that the Syracusans should not have ease to claim their freedom (14.75.1-3); the Corinthians chase the fleeing ships on their own (14.75.5). Stages (history): the route; who goes first (and dies); timing of the fleet; a secret offer after victory.

**Himera, 480** (11.21 to 11.22; Herodotus 7.167). A captured letter, horsemen posing as allies (11.21.4 to 11.22.1); "the battle was swaying back and forth, when suddenly the flames... and sundry persons reported that the general had been slain" (11.22.3); the pursuit ends in surrender to thirst (11.22.4). Herodotus' Carthaginian version differs (7.167.1): show both.

**Cabala and Cronium, c. 383 to 375** (15.15 to 15.17). Magon falls at Cabala and the beaten men are trapped on a hill "without water" (15.15.3). At Cronium Leptines falls: "At his fall the Phoenicians were emboldened" (15.17.1); the winning wing routs when the news spreads (15.17.2); the survivors are "preserved by the coming of night" (15.17.4).

**Thucydides, Syracuse 415 to 413.** Thunder frightens raw troops (6.70.1); the night battle on Epipolae, "how could any one know anything for certain?" (7.44.1); spectators on shore "more overcome than the actual combatants" (7.71.3); Nicias surrenders to stop the slaughter (7.85.1). Gylippus tells beaten men "the fault was not theirs but his" (7.5.3).

**What the texts agree on** (history): most killing comes in the pursuit (11.22.4, 14.60.5, 15.17.3, Thuc. 7.44.8, 7.84); a general's fall breaks a wing (11.22.3, 15.17.1-2); night ends a rout (15.17.4); the commander chooses whether to stop the killing (14.9.5, 14.53.2). Devereaux: mercenary pay "seems to have been due at discharge, at the end of a campaign" (ACOUP, Raising Carthaginian Armies II), and allied contingents were tied to the general, "this attachment is personal" (part III). Devereaux's hoplite-battle series is not saved (parked in `docs/later.md`).

## Assemblies

**The generalship, 406 to 405** (13.91 to 13.96). Silence: "not a man would venture to offer any counsel" (13.91.3); Dionysius accuses the generals, is fined for uproar, and Philistus pays and offers to pay "throughout the whole day" (13.91.4); some generals dismissed (13.92.1); at Gela, the cry for "supreme power", Gelon invoked (13.94.5); "the multitude, as is their wont, swung" (13.95.1); regret after (13.95.2).

**The prisoners, 413** (13.19 to 13.33). Hermocrates shouted down (13.19.5-6); Nicolaus, who "had lost two sons", wins silence (13.19.6, 13.28.1); Gylippus swings it back, "the multitude suddenly changed its mind" (13.33.1).

**Athenagoras and Hermocrates, 415** (Thuc. 6.32 to 6.41). The alarm "concocted by men who are always causing agitation" (6.36.2); a general "stopped any other speakers coming forward" (6.41.1).

**Diocles and Hermocrates, 408 to 407** (13.75). The bones of Himera's dead brought home to shame Diocles; "Diocles was exiled; but even so they did not receive Hermocrates back" (13.75.5). Hermocrates' night entry; he "waited to pick up the late-comers" and was killed (13.75.7-8).

**Theodorus, 396** (14.64 to 14.70). A sea victory won without Dionysius emboldens the citizens (14.64.3-4); Theodorus speaks; "all expected that he would take the lead for liberty" when the Spartan admiral rose (14.70.1); he backed Dionysius and "the mercenaries flocked about Dionysius" (14.70.2); after, "kindly words... gifts... general banquets" (14.70.3).

**Levers attested** (history): silence and fear; a rich friend paying the fines; invoking an ancestor (Gelon); fresh news and a charge of treason; a speaker who wins pity; a foreign ally's word; armed men present; a magistrate closing the debate; gifts and feasts after. **Plausible:** a standing that moves speaker by speaker, since every one of these crowds swings mid-session.

## Plots and crises

**The cavalry revolt, 405** (13.112 to 13.113). The riders meant to kill him on the road, "but when they saw that the mercenaries were not deserting him, they rode off" (13.112.3); they sack his house and abuse his wife (13.112.4); he rides back through the night and burns the Achradina gate (13.113.1); the riders in the market are "shot down to a man" (13.113.2).

**The revolt of 404 to 403** (14.7 to 14.9). Doricus killed at Herbessus (14.7.6-7); the blockade, a bounty, citizenship for deserting mercenaries (14.8.1-3); the council of friends (14.8.5): Heloris, "Tyranny is a fair winding-sheet"; Polyxenus, flee on "your swiftest horse"; Philistus, rather be "cast out, dragged by the leg". He feigns terms and hires the Campanians (14.8.6); the besiegers relax (14.9.1); the rebels split (14.9.4); he stops the killing, "riding among his men" (14.9.5); amnesty, which some refuse (14.9.6-8).

**Disarming, c. 403** (14.10.4): the Syracusans sent out "to harvest their crops", and their arms taken. **Pay, 396** (14.78.1-3): Aristotle arrested; the men "demanded their pay with some sharpness"; paid in land at Leontini.

**Dion's fall, 354** (Plutarch, *Dion* 54 to 57; outside the decade, a model only). Callippus sounds out malcontents as if spying for Dion; Dion would rather die than live on guard against his friends (56); the oath, then the blow at the festival of Kore; "no one ventured to open the door" (57).

**Stages attested in order** (history): grumbling; a spark; the night; a council of three advisers; a feint; outside help; a split among the plotters; mercy or killing.

## Eight scene seeds

1. The Gela plan (405): columns that must arrive, then the night council.
2. The counterstroke at Syracuse (396): route, the wing to sacrifice, the fleet's timing, the secret offer.
3. The winding-sheet council (404): three friends, three roads; feint, outside help, the split.
4. The ride from Gela (405): the riders on the road, the race home, the burning gate.
5. Theodorus' assembly (396): the speech gains ground until the ally rises.
6. The paid fine (406 to 405): silence, accusation, a rich backer, the vote.
7. Motya's night ladders (397): a habit, then the night; then the sack.
8. Cronium (c. 383 to 375): a wing commander falls; save the line or yourself; night.

## Passages used by the plot, crisis and debate scenes (test 5 build, thread 49)

- Unrest, 404: "The revolters also proclaimed a large reward to any who would slay the tyrant and promised citizenship to any mercenaries who would come over to them" (14.8.3).

- Mercy after: "Dionysius treated with humanity the exiles who returned" (14.9.8).
- The debate levers: Philistus "paid the fine and urged Dionysius to speak out" (13.91.4); "when Gelon was general with supreme power" (13.94.5); Pharacidas came "not to overthrow the rule of Dionysius", and "the mercenaries flocked about Dionysius" (14.70.2); "honouring some of them with gifts and inviting some to general banquets" (14.70.3).
