# What the first tests taught us (2026-11-26)

Thread 35. One overview of Jacob's own game and the AI testers' games, asked for by Jacob: "reports/suggestions/overview of everything we learned from my test game and the AI tester games". **Report only: nothing here is built.** Rule changes and direction are Jacob's call.

## In five lines

1. **The gate is not passed.** Ten seasons gave no story he would retell: "No one or nothing stood out."
2. **The screen buried the game.** He read what has structure (ledger, offices, factions, events, map) and skipped prose and people lists. The biggest event of his game, the assembly taking his generalship, went by unnoticed.
3. **It was too easy, through two loopholes** the bots had already found: free taxes once a faction hates you, and the same action on the same man every season.
4. **The bots predicted all of this** (flat first decades, overload, the levy loophole), but could not say what would make it good. Only his play did.
5. **His direction answers the gate:** a small cast that rises in importance, fighting over the offices as the board.

## What worked (keep it)

- **Events.** "Liked the events." The Spartan envoy, the captain's dream, the fire in Achradina, the man with the secret. Every AI tester also named the events as the part that reads as story.
- **The map and the war.** "Liked the map and geopolitics/war even if it is very very simple." "The army part was kinda fun." The AI testers called the island tab the best screen.
- **A rival you watch rise.** Nicoteles, the citizens' leader, "looked like a threat that was cooking up". It was the one thread he followed, even without understanding influence.
- **Things with holders.** He used the offices and the faction cards. Objects that someone holds read at a glance.
- **The look.** Parchment, the season card, the map: no tester and no note complained.
- **The engine.** 1,008 games, no crash, no broken state; his save replays to the same digest.

## What the tests taught us about testing

- **The bots were right about the problems.** Flat first decades (33% with no story shape), the levy loophole, repeated text, overload: all four showed up in his game.
- **The bots could not see what mattered.** They never miss a log line, so none could notice the generalship loss going unnoticed. None farmed one man for revenge or fun. None could say "I liked the events".
- **AI testers overstate quitting, but not overload.** The newcomer lens said "I would have quit around here". He didn't quit, but he glazed over the same parts.
- **We built faster than he could play.** 17 switches and eight bets, and his game touched none of them. The council's warning (Part B) came true: the bets cannot be sorted from play yet.
- **Keep the bots as smoke detectors** (crashes, loops, free actions, repeated lines), and let his games judge fun.
