# Round 1: The Player's Advocate

## What I saw at the keyboard (built the prototype in a scratch copy and played it headless at 1400 px)

- **Season 1 shows about 1,400 words and about 80 buttons**: 28 action types, the six offices, four faction agenda tracks, a six-step guide, two plots ("source unknown") and two enemies who "hate you", all before your first move. Too much for a novice.
- **Doing nothing gets you exiled in season 4** (summer 404). The guard turned kingmaker, refused a duty, and my defense fell to 4. Nothing on the screen said "this is the one thing that matters right now".
- **Repetition shows up early.** "Stories about you spread among the rich families" appeared three seasons in a row, and so did Leptines' petition for the generalship.
- **What works:** every roll is a sentence, every change has a reason, the plot track is clear, and the parchment look is calm. The bones are good. The danger is that Phase 2 puts a map on top of a screen that is already full.

My rule for the council: **every island feature has to replace or compress something on the screen, not sit beside it.** A season should still be: read what moved, answer, take three actions, end. That's about 5 to 10 clicks.

## Q1. The first slice of the island

**Position.** The smallest island is **Syracuse plus four places**: Leontini, Catana or Naxos (pick one), Aetna (the exiles' camp), and Carthage's corner of the island shown as one region. No more. The span should be **about 16 seasons (four years)**, so a first game fits in one or two evenings. Jacob chooses the years; I'd lean toward the run-up to the great war, because most of the sourced events date from 398 to 396 and players rarely reach them now. The map is not a second game. It is **where your dangers live**: exiles, rivals, armies and Carthage's envoys each have a place.

**Proposal: one new decision each season, "Where are you?"** In the city, or with the army. That is the hinge that turns two games into one loop. Leave, and you can act on the map (march, besiege, win glory), but your defense drops and plotters at home gain a season. Stay, and someone else commands, and a general who wins abroad comes home popular. This is history, not invention: while Dionysius besieged Herbessus, his own army revolted. Diodorus 14.7.6 (`sources/diodorus/english/book14-1-18-97-117.loeb.txt`): "the Syracusans who were in the army, now that they had arms in their hands, began to gather in groups and upbraid each other that they had not joined with the cavalry in overthrowing the tyrant"; then 14.8.1: "Dionysius, terror-stricken at the revolt of the Syracusans, broke off the siege and hastened to Syracuse."

**Refuse.** A separate map screen with its own turn, or map actions that don't cost one of your three actions.

## Q2. Simulated Carthage

**Position.** Make it lighter for the first test: **three named men, one visible goal, a mood, and a debate you can see**. Carthage's depth is worth building only when the player can feel it. A full copy of Syracuse's rules running behind a curtain is a designer's pleasure and invisible to the player.

**Proposal: a "Carthage debates" line each season, in the same Whispers-Rumors-Named style as the plots.** For example: "In the council at Carthage, the war party gains ground. Himilco's name is spoken." Make the mirror concrete: Carthage's men make offers to *your* rivals, and your exiles go to *them*. There is already a sourced "Carthage's secret offer" event to build on.

**Refuse.** Carthaginian factions, offices and plots in the first slice. Also refuse any Carthage the player only meets as a number going up.

## Q3. Armies and war

**Position.** An army is **a named general plus a strength**, shown in words ("about 3,000 men under Leptines"). A battle is **one decision before one roll with shown odds**: fight, pull back, or bribe or parley. A siege is **a four-step track**, like the plot track, so players read it the same way. Mercenary arrears already drive the best stories in the prototype, so extend them: unpaid soldiers in the field desert or seize the town they hold.

**Proposal: war's main output is domestic.** Every battle result moves a person: the general gains or loses influence, the losing side's men get grudges, and the dead leave widows and brothers (enemies with a reason, like the brother of Demarchus now). The chronicle should say "Leptines comes home from Catana a hero, and the guard cheers him, not you."

**Refuse.** Supply chains, unit types and tactical battles in the first slice. Supply is parked, and it should stay parked.

## Q4. The other cities

**Position.** Give each city **one leader by name, one stance toward you (friend, wary, hostile, yours), and one thing it has** (grain, men, ships or a harbour). No city factions. What makes a city matter to the player is who is there.

**Proposal: cities are where people go.** Exiles flee to Aetna or Rhegium, rivals take refuge there, and a city that shelters your enemy becomes a reason to march. Taking a city should mean choosing what to do with the people in it: resettle them, install a friend, or sell them. Diodorus has the resettlement: Leontini "left their city, and removed to Syracuse" (14.15.4, same file).

**Refuse.** Per-city economies with more than one number.

## Q5. Plots and the sword

**Position.** The worst thing for a player is not dying. It's dying in year 2 to a blow you couldn't answer, or living through a decade where nothing struck. **Method-and-moment plots give the player a choice at the climax**, and that's where the tension should peak. Partial success (wounded, fled, a brother killed in your place) keeps the story going after its best scene.

**Proposal: when a plot is Named, it also shows its moment** ("at the festival", "when you ride out to the army", "at your brother's wedding"). You get one climax decision: skip it, double the guard, walk in as bait, or strike first. Being away on campaign should be the obvious moment, which ties Q5 to Q1. For the balance package, test "Reap what you sow" first, as the sweep recommends, because careful play is rewarded there (82%) and careless play is not (0%). Jacob will feel that his choices count.

**Refuse.** Fair fight as the first package: about two thirds of strikes become coin flips, and to a novice that reads as random.

## Q6. Rivals and nemeses

**Position.** At the moment, nemeses almost never appear and the same man becomes your enemy five or six times. Players remember **one antagonist per campaign**, the way you remember a Shadow of Mordor orc. Rank-ups by count don't make that kind of man. Survival does.

**Proposal: a rival becomes a Nemesis by escaping you,** not by four rank-ups: a plot leader who survives a failed strike, a man you exiled who comes back with an army, or a general who beats you in the field. He gets a title in the chronicle ("Hipparinus the Returned"), an address on the map, and escalation instead of repetition (ask, demand, act, flee, return). Exile becomes a cliffhanger, not an ending.

**Refuse.** Political rivals flickering on and off. Once someone becomes a rival, he stays one until something happens to him.

## Q7. The player's own goal

**Position.** Survival alone makes you purely reactive, and Jacob liked plots that came from *his* actions. Give him a want.

**Proposal: pick one of three ambitions at the start, each a visible four-step agenda like a faction's.** For example: build the citadel and the great walls, found a dynasty by marriage, or drive Carthage from a city. At the end, the prize is **the chronicle plus a title earned** ("Dionysius the Builder, who died at the festival"), not a score.

**Refuse.** A points total. It pulls play toward a number and away from the story.

## Q8. Voices and legibility

**Position.** For "a story you would retell", what matters most is **the chronicle grouped by storyline**, then **warnings built from what the plotters really did**. The council of advisors is the riskiest of the three: it adds words to a screen that already shows 1,400.

**Proposal: set a word budget.** The season screen stays under about 1,000 words even after the island arrives. If a council comes, it *replaces* existing text: the "what you can do" line becomes Philistus saying it, and Heloris disagreeing. Also show "the one thing that matters this season" at the top (the guard unpaid, a plot Named), because I lost a game that way.

**Refuse.** Any voice feature that adds a panel.

## Q9. Architecture and risk (the player's half)

**Position.** The biggest risk is not the code. It's that **no human has passed the Phase 1 gate** ("ten seasons produce a story you would retell"), and the island adds a second unknown on top of the first. If the first island test is dull, nobody will know which half is to blame.

**Proposal: a named "first test" preset** that fixes, in data, which of thread 09's switches are on, which balance package applies, and which scenario. Jacob shouldn't be testing a configuration nobody chose, and when he reports "it felt X", we need to know which game he played. Keep "citadel" as the tutorial scenario. Add a headless bot that goes to war, so the 100-seed check covers the island too.

**Refuse.** Shipping the island with every switch off by default and calling that the test. Defaults-off is right for rules, but the test needs a deliberate set.

## My top 3 for the foundation

1. **Q1: the "Where are you?" choice.** It decides whether this is one game or two.
2. **Q5: a climax decision at the moment of the strike.** That's where tension and agency meet.
3. **Q6: a nemesis made by escape and return.** That's the story people retell, and the island gives it somewhere to go.

## The idea nobody else will raise: a "mark this moment" button

The game is deterministic: a game is a seed plus Jacob's inputs. So add a small button that lets him tag a moment while playing ("confused", "unfair", "great", "bored"). The tag goes into the input list, and the export is one short line he can paste into chat. Claude then replays his exact game headless and reads the season he flagged, instead of guessing from a description. This turns every playtest into data without asking a novice to write a report. It's cheap and doesn't touch the rules.

*Prototype observations come from one run (default seed, standard package, no actions taken), so they are not a sample.*
