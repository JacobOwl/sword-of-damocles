# Round 2: The Player's Advocate

I accept the chair's summary of where we agree. My test for every dispute below: what does Jacob see, what does he choose, and does it fit on one season's screen?

## D1. Map and span
**Position.** Run **405 to 395** with **five places**: Syracuse, Aetna, Leontini, Catana and the Punic west (Motya). The first test should pause at the end of year 4 and ask "continue?", with the chronicle so far on screen.
**Moved by:** the Skeptic and the Devil. Keeping Phase 1's decade means the island is the only new thing, so when Jacob says "it felt X", we know which half he means. I drop my 399 start.
**Not moved:** the Historian's eight places. Eight cities on top of 24 people is more names than a newcomer can hold. Still, I'd take his treaty standings as the *words* for each city's relation to you ("tributary, unwalled", "lives under its own laws").
**Vote:** five places, 405 to 395, pause after year 4.

## D2. The hinge
**Position.** Combine three of them. Map moves cost the same three action slots (d). Soldiers come from one shared pool of men, which serves as either your defense or your army (a). Each campaign forces one visible choice: **go yourself and name a regent, or send a commission under a named man** (b and f together). That's one decision with two faces. Going yourself brings glory, and the regent carries your sword check. Sending a man keeps you safe, but he's the one who comes home popular.
**Moved by:** the Systems seat's shared pool. "Your defense: 7, falls to 4 if you march" is a single number moving on screen. The Devil's commission turned my "where are you" into a real dilemma, where before it was a toggle.
**Refuse:** (e) fixed away and home seasons, because they take the choice away from the player. (c) as a separate rule is unnecessary, since March already needs the generalship.
**Vote:** a + b + d + f.

## D3. Supply
**Position.** Supply earns one counter in the first slice: **seasons of food the army carries**, shown on the army ("food for two seasons"). At zero, the army must come home, forage (a roll that angers the land it eats), or start to desert. There are no chains, routes or depots.
**Moved by:** the Historian. A retreat "because the transport of his food supplies was reduced" (14.55.5) is the kind of decision a player feels, and one number covers it.
**Vote:** one food counter per army, nothing more.

## D4. Faction power
**Position.** Replace the drift toward 5. Factions became Marginal only 0.04 times a game in the old runs, so the rise and fall Jacob asked for never shows. Power should change only for a named reason: an office won or lost, a victory, a defeat, plunder shared or withheld, or a leader killed or exiled. It should show **as a man first and a number second**: "Nicoteles can fill the assembly (power 7)."
**Moved by:** the Systems seat on the cause and the Devil on the display.
**Vote:** sources and sinks instead of drift; lead with the named man.

## D5. Balance package and preset
**Position.** Use **Reap what you sow**. Fair fight's coin-flip strikes will feel random to a novice before he has learned to read the warnings. Systems is right that they make more survivors, though. Once Jacob trusts the warnings, try Fair fight as the *second* test.
**Preset:** yes. The Devil and the Skeptic arrived at the same thing (named configurations), so I count that as agreement. "First island test" should name its scenario, its package and which of thread 09's switches are on. Of those switches, I'd turn on clue warnings and partial success, and leave the rest off.
**Vote:** Reap what you sow, inside a named preset.

## D6. Rivals, merged into one rule
**Proposal: every man has one feud ledger with you.** Entries never vanish (Narrative). A pardon, a favour or a marriage adds a chapter that *converts* the feud into a debt or a tie, but it doesn't erase it (Systems). **Surviving you twice** makes him a Nemesis (Devil and me), where surviving means escaping an arrest, living through a failed strike, or coming back from exile. The same ledger is what the storyline chronicle reads, so one record serves three seats.
**Nicoteles.** Leave him in as a possible main threat, but don't let him be the threat in most games. The player's reason is the second campaign: if every game ends with Nicoteles and the assembly, nobody starts another one. Thread 09's switch is the right place for this, so the fix doesn't need its own rule.
**Vote:** the merged ledger; Nicoteles stays, but not every time.

## D7. Carthage's inner life
**Position.** Use the Historian's **general who must give an account at home**. A named man who fears judgment is a better story than an invented war party versus peace party, and it's sourced: Himilco "passed sentence of death upon himself" (14.76.4). Add one visible mood for the council at home.
**On the Devil's charge:** half right. The *mechanism* (Carthage's politics react to your results) is fine. *Calling* it a mirror in the UI or the docs frames the game around a thesis, and CLAUDE.md warns against that. Build the mechanism, drop the label.
**Vote:** the general's account plus one mood, with no "mirror" framing.

## D8. Ranking by value for cost
1. **Story sweep** (Narrative): cheap and very high value. Jacob judges ten stories in five minutes.
2. **"Mark this moment" button** (mine): cheap and high value. It turns his real play into replayable evidence.
3. **Losing Syracuse as a turn, not the end** (Devil): highest value, but costly. Don't build it in the first slice. Do give exiles a *place* now, so it's possible later. Park it in `later.md` for Jacob.
4. **One dice stream per system** (Skeptic): medium value and cheap code, but it changes a CLAUDE.md rule and every pinned digest. Jacob's call, and it is worth asking him before thread 10 adds island rolls, not after.
5. **Disputed numbers from rival historians** (Historian): lovely and sourced, but it adds words to the screen. Park it for the history-browser phase.

## The verdict I'd fight hardest for
**An island season is still one screen and the same three actions. The island adds exactly one new decision, go or send (with a regent or a commissioned man), and every map element replaces text rather than adding a panel.** I'd add a word budget too: under about 1,000 words a season, checked by a test the way "no code ids" is checked today.

**My dissent if the council goes the other way:** if the island ships as a separate map turn, or adds its panels on top of the current screen, then the first test will measure Jacob's patience, not whether the game is fun. A dull or confusing first island game would then be blamed on the design, when the cause is the interface. The handoff should record this risk.
