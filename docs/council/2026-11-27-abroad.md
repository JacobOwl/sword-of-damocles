## In five lines

1. **Carthage loses battles, not wars.** After a lost army it goes home, mends, courts your towns and the Sicels, and comes back bigger, as it did in 393 (Diodorus 14.90, 14.95). The return is driven by what you hold, never by a date.
2. **Carthage's council debates in the open.** Once a year, and after each blow, its blocs argue war or peace in a short scene with named speakers. You can tip the debate, at a price.
3. **Three friends and one rival across the sea, all from 398 to 396:** Sparta (a patron whose help comes with a man who can veto you), Corinth (ships when begged), Locri (a bride's city) and Rhegium (the rival across the Strait, now a real town on the map).
4. **Help is an object you can lose.** Spartan men and ships serve while you stay in Sparta's favour (Pax Pamir's loyalty rule) and leave if you lose it.
5. **The Italian cities wake late.** If you push into Italy or hold the Strait, the Italian Greek league forms against you (14.91.1, about 393), Here I Stand style: the minors sleep until you poke them.

## What all four seats agreed

- **No relation numbers, no treaty screen, no points currency.** Words for moods (Total War: Rome II shows attitude as four words), a few fixed offers, and the cost is people, silver, ships and time.
- **No friend that is only good.** Sparta "in truth" came to "increase the power of the tyranny" (14.10.2), and its admiral vetoed the Syracusans' bid for liberty (14.70.1 to 14.70.3). Every gift arrives with a man, a debt or a grudge.
- **No popup per power.** At most one foreign matter a season, among the matters you already read. Arrivals are announced a season ahead (CK2 Sunset Invasion's explorers before the fleet; the sword rule).
- **No Athens, Persia or Xenophon** until a text is saved. Nothing in the saved texts ties Syracuse to them in 398 to 395.
- **Carthage is never a win button.** Neither Agathocles nor Regulus took it.

## Where they disagreed, and the call

- **Diplomacy shape.** The strategy seat: attitude words for every power plus a favour pool for Sparta (CK2 Jade Dragon's "grace"). The board seat: Pax Pamir patron loyalty for Sparta and Corinth, sleeping minors for Italy (Here I Stand), and no influence board (it would duplicate the town houses). The player seat: three or four cards with a mood and two actions. *Call: cards with a mood word (all), Sparta's help as objects you keep only while in favour (Pax Pamir, board seat), Italy asleep until woken (board seat). No pool number on screen; favour is the mood word.*
- **Where the powers sit.** Edge cards (strategy, player), homeland boxes on the map margin with pieces that turn face up (board seat, Time of Crisis), envoys with travel time (historian). *Call: Rhegium on the map as a town; the others as cards on the map's edge at the end of their sea lane, each card showing pieces (Spartan ships, the envoy away) the Time of Crisis way.*
- **Libya in 398.** Strategy and board seats would allow a what-if raid now; the historian and player seats say keep it for later. *Jacob decided: in test 5, built last (Spec 3), labelled a what-if.*

## Spec 1. Carthage comes back (switch `carthageReturns`)

**The history.** After the disaster of 396 Carthage did not quit. Magon "set about retrieving the Carthaginian cause after the disaster they had suffered, for he showed kindness to the subject cities and received the victims of Dionysius' wars. He also formed alliances with most of the Siceli" (14.90.2 to 14.90.3, about 393). Then "the Carthaginians, after a slow recovery from the disaster they had suffered at Syracuse, resolved to keep their hand in Sicilian affairs", raising "troops from Libya and Sardinia as well as from the barbarians of Italy", "no less than eighty thousand" under Mago (14.95.1). Even in the first despair "they constructed ships and made careful provision of supplies for the war" (14.77.5). The war of 393 to 392 ended in a treaty, not a conquest (14.96.3 to 14.96.4). Files: `phoenician-punic-world/sources/diodorus/english/book14-78-96.txt`, `book14.loeb.txt`.

**The games.** Republic of Rome: wars are cards from outside that wait and come back; an ignored war adds unrest every turn (rules 1.07.331, 1.08.11, verified by the board seat). Herman's *Peloponnesian War*: the bot is driven by "bellicosity", and a defeat costs a full point while a win adds half (verified). EU4: war exhaustion from "defeats, blocked harbors, wear and war taxes" (verified); the truce after a peace is from memory, unverified. CK2 Sunset Invasion: a warning arrival before the fleet (verified).

### 1a. A lost army starts a recovery, not a peace

Today (`src/core/carthage.ts` `prefers`): the Elders want peace once one army is lost, the Hundred once two are. With the switch:
- When a Carthaginian army is destroyed or Carthage makes peace, Carthage's goal becomes **Recover** for `recoverSeasons` (knob, say 4 to 6). Recover is the existing `rebuild` priority list plus `court` (Magon's kindness to subject towns, 14.90.3) and a new act **Take in the exiles** (Carthage welcomes men you exiled or purged; each one raises its courting chance in his home town; 14.90.3 "received the victims of Dionysius' wars").
- `armiesLost` no longer locks the Elders on peace forever: it fades by one every `forgetSeasons` (knob). A lost army is a scar, not a veto.
- While recovering, Carthage's anger at you does not fall below its level at the peace. It rises with what you take (the existing anger sources) and with **what you hold** (the pendulum, `war.ts` `pendulum`).
- A treaty gives a **truce** of `truceSeasons` (knob) during which Carthage will not vote war unless you break the terms (take a town the treaty left to it). From memory, unverified: EU4's truce. History: the peace of 392 held for several years (15.15 is the next war, about 383).

### 1b. The council debates (a scene, once a year and after each blow)

**The history.** Carthage's war letter of 398/397 "had been read in the council and subsequently before the people" (14.47.2). Aristotle's rule, already in the game: if the kings and the Elders agree, the matter is theirs; if not, the people decide (*Politics* 1273a, written about 340 to 330, so a later description). After a landing the council "reprimanded all the commanders of the fleet" and named "Hanno and Bormilcar, men who had an inherited feud" (20.10.1, 310 BC, later but the same council). The game: Victoria 3's diplomatic play (verified: no declare-war button, a demand, a countdown, each side sways the undecided).

The rule:
- **When:** each spring, and in the season after Carthage loses an army, a town of its own, or makes peace.
- **What:** each voting bloc (Elders, Hundred, people) names a speaker from Carthage's cast (`data/carthage.json` people; invented elders are marked invention) and takes a stance word: *for war*, *for waiting*, *for peace*. The reason is the one `prefers` already gives ("a Punic town has fallen", "Carthage is weary of the war"). The vote is decided as now (Aristotle). This adds no new rule to the vote; it shows it.
- **The scene:** one big event scene (thread 46's format, `docs/council/2026-11-27-big-scenes.md`): three speakers, their stance, the outcome, and who decided ("the people's assembly, since the Elders were against it"). Desktop and phone. No choice for you inside it; your levers come before it.
- **Your levers (actions, each one season ahead):**
  - **Send a herald to Carthage** (14.47.1 to 14.47.2: "a letter to the senate"). A demand. If the blocs are split, it moves the people's bloc one step toward war (a demand insults) or, if you offer terms, the Elders one step toward waiting. Victoria 3's demand.
  - **Pay a man in Carthage** (14.75.1 to 14.75.3, the secret bargain with Himilcon; Carthage already does this to you with "Send silver to the tyrant's enemies"). Silver to one speaker moves his stance one step. Found out (the existing secrets roll), it angers all three blocs.
  - **Spare them** (14.75.3: Dionysius let the citizens go "in order that the Syracusans, by reason of their fear of the Carthaginians, should never find a time of ease"). After a win, letting a beaten army sail home lowers Carthage's anger but raises fear at home: no peace dividend in your own city. Already half there as the war card's terms; this only names it.

### 1c. The second expedition

- When the council votes war after a recovery, the expedition hires on the pendulum's scale (existing `hireBonus`) and its army is drawn from **Libya, Sardinia and Italy** (14.95.1) as named contingents, so thread 47's contingents apply to Carthage too. If its citizens' contingent is destroyed, thread 47's rule holds (the council swears off risking citizens, 16.81.3 to 16.81.4); in the next debate the Elders speak for hiring, not levying. The Libyan bloc's mood falls with each levy (Devereaux: Libyan levies were "paid conscripts", `acoup/raising-carthaginian-armies-2.devereaux.md`; older scholars call them mercenaries; show both).
- It is **announced a season ahead** (existing war-card landing notice), with the general's name and the contingents. Sunset Invasion's warning.
- A new general each time, with standing (thread 41's "generals with standing"). Magon for the first return (14.90, 14.95); invented men after him.
- **Unrest from an unanswered landing** (Republic of Rome 1.08.11): each season Carthage's army stands on your island and you have not fought or besieged it, the people's mood falls a step. Dionysius twice lost his citizens by refusing battle (14.61.3, 14.96.2). The war council already found "refusing battle should cost allies and patience".

### 1d. What to count (not tune)

`npm run war-runs -- --preset <test 5> --seeds 300`: how many games see a second landing within 20 seasons; seasons from first peace to second landing; how often Carthage never returns (broken if it is 0% or 100%); how often the debate scene fires (should be about once a year, not every season). Report; don't tune to 393.

### 2b. The actions (each a season; each can be refused; each answer is one line, not a popup)

1. **Send an envoy** to a power. A named man of your cast is away for one or two seasons (off your council, out of reach of plots), and the reply comes when he returns. Polyxenus went "to the Greeks in Italy and to the Lacedaemonians, as well as the Corinthians" (14.62.1) and came back "bringing thirty warships" (14.63.4). Games: EU4's diplomat who is busy while posted; Old World's ambassador (both verified by the strategy seat). The envoy is the precondition for 3 and 5.
2. **Offer a marriage** to Locri or Rhegium. The town's assembly votes, using its three houses (`townBoards`). Rhegium "voted not to accept"; Locri approved, Doris (14.44.5 to 14.44.6). A refusal is remembered as a grudge with its reason: the later siege of Rhegium came "because of the slight he had received in connection with his offer of marriage" (14.106.1). CK3 marriage alliances (verified). The existing event `rhegiumRefuses` becomes the answer to this action instead of a random event.
3. **Ask for help** (Sparta or Corinth, with an envoy there). If the mood is high enough, ships or men arrive one or two seasons later as **allied squadrons** that join your fleet as a second part with their own heart (thread 47's navy, `docs/council/2026-11-27-units-ground-navy.md` Part 3), or as a land contingent. Sparta's comes with **Pharacidas** (14.63.4), a person in your court. Games: Rome II and EU4's call to arms (verified); Pax Pamir's patriots serve only while your loyalty holds (verified).
4. **Give a gift** (silver, grain, or land to Messana or Locri). Moves the mood one step. "He made a present to the Messenians of a large piece of territory on their borders, binding them to him by such a benefaction" (14.44.4). Rome II and Imperator gifts.
5. **Ask leave to recruit in Laconia** (Sparta at Friendly or better). Unlocks a pricier mercenary band with its own captain: Sparta "gave him permission to enlist as many mercenaries from them as he might wish" (14.44.2); "over a thousand mercenaries from the Lacedaemonians" (14.58.1). Imperator's mercenaries: dearer than your own men, their own general, a risk of betrayal (verified).

### 2c. Sparta, the patron with strings

- **Help is objects.** Pharacidas, the Spartan squadron and the Laconian band each serve while Sparta's mood is Friendly or Patron. If it falls below, they sail home at the end of the season, with a warning a season before. Pax Pamir: switching loyalty means you "first return your gifts" (verified).
- **What raises Sparta's mood:** gifts; keeping order at home (no revolt this year); not settling Messenian exiles (14.78.5, Sparta "offended"). **What lowers it:** a revolt you lose, a liberty vote you allow, honouring Messenians.
- **The veto.** Once while Pharacidas is in Syracuse, when a liberty matter or assembly revolt comes up, he speaks for you and blocks it (14.70.1 to 14.70.3): it fails, you keep power, and the citizens' mood falls two steps (history: the Syracusans remembered that "Aretes the Lacedaemonian, at the time that he was asserting the right of the Syracusans to freedom, had betrayed them", 14.70.3; the mood step is invention). A big scene for thread 46. You cannot refuse the veto; that is the price of the patron.
- **Herman's rule:** a defeat with Spartan ships in your fleet costs Sparta's mood a full step; a win raises it half a step (two wins for one step).

### 2d. The second front: Rhegium and the Italian cities

- **Rhegium is a town on the map**, with a sea lane to Messana (thread 47's "Carry the army" order applies) and the exile scenario's Rhegium stays as it is. Its houses lean against you at the start (14.40: in 399 it "sent out ... six thousand infantry, six hundred cavalry, and fifty triremes" against you).
- **Rhegium's war party can march without a vote** (14.40.4 to 14.40.5): if Rhegium is Hostile and its leading house is strong, it may march with Messana's generals; Messana's men can turn back home if Messana's people did not vote for it ("since the people had not approved the war"). This is an existing town-board check, not a new system.
- **The league wakes** (Here I Stand 9.6, verified: war on a minor brings in its patron; Republic of Rome's inactive wars that a matching card activates). The league card is face down. It turns face up when you attack Rhegium or Locri's enemies, or hold Messana and Rhegium both for a year. Then the Italian Greeks "formed an alliance among themselves and established a Council" (14.91.1) with the mutual-aid rule: "if any city's territory was being plundered ... they should all come to its aid" (14.101.1). A league army can land at Rhegium or Messana, announced a season ahead. Historically about 393, so in a 20-season game from 398 it would come near the end if you pushed.
- **Carthage and the second front together.** Carthage's return (Spec 1) and the league can both be live. That is the difficulty Jacob asked for. Count how often a game has both, and how often it kills the player.

### 2e. How it looks

- **Map:** Rhegium as a ninth town across the Strait (check its position against a saved map source before drawing; the player seat gave about 15.65 E, 38.1 N from memory). Sea lanes running off the map's east edge end in small cards: Locri, Corinth, Sparta, the league. Each card: a coin or emblem from the art library (`assets/library/catalog.json`; no city coins until checked against a saved source), the mood word, the one wish, and pieces on it (your envoy, a Spartan squadron waiting). Time of Crisis' homeland boxes (verified). Libya stays the existing Carthage point.
- **Phone:** the cards stack under the map, folded to name and mood.
- **No numbers on the cards.** Mood as words; an offer shows its chance in the existing "4 in 6" style (Total War: Troy shows a chance before you send, verified).

### 2f. What to count

Per 300 games of the test 5 preset: how often each action is used; how often Pharacidas' veto fires; how often Sparta's help leaves; how often the league wakes; deaths in games with Carthage's return and the league both live. Broken: an action no bot ever uses, or one that wins almost always.

## Spec 3. Carry the war to Libya (switch `libyaCrossing`)

**Jacob changed the plan:** "no I want version with everything ... I will wait if needed". So the invasion is in test 5, built **after** Specs 1 and 2 and after thread 47's navy. It needs `armySupply`, thread 47's fleet and "Carry the army" order, the commission switch's keeper, and Spec 1's debate scene.

**It is a what-if for 398 to 395.** No Syracusan army went to Libya in these years (none in the saved texts). The screen says so once, when the action first appears: "Dionysius never did this. Agathocles did, in 310." The models are Agathocles (310 to 307, Diodorus book 20) and Regulus (256 to 255, Polybius 1.26 to 1.34), and the Libyans' hatred of Carthage (20.55.4; 14.77.1, saved in Greek only). Both invasions began well and ended badly. That is the shape: a gamble that can win a war and lose a city.

**The games** (checked by the seats, links in their statements): Hannibal & Hamilcar 11.5 (a sea move rolls against the enemy's navy), 19.4 (control that cannot trace to your army is removed) and 20.1.1 (a siege needs points); Nevsky 4.7.3 (ships per load of food); Republic of Rome 1.10.11 (no land battle without enough fleets); Total War: Rome II (fragile transports, escorts take the hit); EU4 (wear beyond supply); Victoria 3 (the main demand); Oath (the Chancellor and the exile, for the keeper). ### 3b. Going: "Carry the war to Libya"

- **Needs:** an army with food, your fleet with squadrons at least the army's strength ÷ 2 (thread 47's "Carry the army" rule), and **a keeper named for Syracuse**. Agathocles "set up his brother Antander as curator of the city with an adequate garrison" (20.4.1), and told no one first (20.4.1: "Disclosing this intention to none of his friends").
- **The crossing:** from Syracuse to the Quarries, about six days at sea (20.6.1: "six days and the same number of nights"). If Carthage's fleet watches the lane, thread 47's interception and sea battle come first. If Syracuse is blockaded, the crossing is the breakout (20.5.3 to 20.5.4: the Carthaginians chased Agathocles and the grain ships got in).
- **Food goes by ship** (Nevsky 4.7.3): the months of food you take need hulls; whatever does not fit stays home.
- **Leaving is a secret.** The order is shown as "your army sails" to the council only once it is at sea, so the keeper and your rivals learn of it a season late. History: 20.4.1. Game: none needed; it is the existing hidden-move rule for your own army.

### 3c. Landing: burn the ships?

A choice on landing, one scene (thread 46):
- **Beach them:** the fleet stays at the Quarries. It guards the landing and is your way home, but caught beached it gives the enemy +2 (thread 47's rule).
- **Burn them as a vow** (20.7.1 to 20.7.5): the army's heart rises two steps ("in victory alone would they have hope of safety", 20.7.5), and you cannot leave Libya without new ships (see 3g). The next season the army's heart drops a step as "they considered the vastness of the sea that separated them from home" (20.8.1), unless it wins or plunders a town that season (20.8.5 to 20.8.6).

### 3d. In Libya

- **Living off the land:** Ravage (from `armySupply`) in the farmland gives food and plunder, as Agathocles' army did at Megalepolis ("he at a single stroke loaded his army with booty", 20.8.6). Each season away, the army wears down one step unless it took a town (EU4's wear beyond supply).
- **Open towns:** Libya's towns have weak or no walls ("not secured with walls ... but lay in level plains", Justin 22.5.5; Diodorus has real sieges too, 20.55). A town falls by storm, or comes over "because of fear, others because of their hatred for the Carthaginians" (20.17.1). Use the existing going-over roll with a "hates Carthage" bonus for Libyan towns.
- **Held only while you are near:** a town that came over goes back to Carthage if your army is more than a set number of days away (Hannibal & Hamilcar 19.4). Agathocles razed his first two towns rather than garrison them (20.8.7): offer **Raze** as an alternative (plunder now, no town to hold).
- **The Libyans:** each Libyan town that comes over lowers Carthage's Libyan bloc a step. At Angry, the existing Libyan revolt starts, and a **Libyan contingent** may join you (thread 47's contingent, low loyalty): Aelymas "the king of the Libyans" joined and then "deserted him" (20.18.3). Its loyalty falls after any lost battle.

### 3e. Carthage's answer (Spec 1's debate, forced)

The season after you land, the council debates (Spec 1b) with three new stances:
- **Recall the army from Sicily.** Agathocles' reasoning: "they would not continue to besiege Syracuse, when they were suffering from a siege of their own city" (Justin 22.5.10). In 310 they sent to Hamilcar in Sicily "begging him to send aid as soon as possible" (20.15.1). Carthage's army in Sicily sails home; a second blow later may come.
- **Arm the citizens.** "They led the citizen soldiers themselves into the field, in number not less than forty thousand foot-soldiers" (20.10.5). A Carthaginian citizens' army appears near Carthage, with thread 47's citizens' contingent rule (if destroyed, the council swears off risking citizens).
- **Strike Syracuse while he is away.** Keep or send an army to besiege Syracuse; the keeper must hold.
- **Two generals with a feud.** The council names two (20.10.1: "Hanno and Bormilcar, men who had an inherited feud"). If one is ambitious and Carthage is losing, he may try to seize Carthage (20.44.1: Bormilcar "declared himself tyrant"; it failed and he was killed, 20.44.6). One event, rare, from the state: a Carthage in civil strife skips its next act.
- The council "reprimanded all the commanders of the fleet" (20.10.1): Carthage's admiral loses standing if you crossed unopposed.

### 3f. Carthage itself

- **Never stormed.** You can besiege it. Each season of siege adds to Carthage's weariness (the war card) and lowers the Elders' mood. Refugees crowd in and cause "a general scarcity" (20.59.2). No siege points ever complete. Hannibal & Hamilcar makes Carthago a sudden-death win; here it is the opposite, because no one took it before 146.
- **Peace from Libya:** with Carthage weary, the existing terms apply, larger. **Ask too much and it refuses** (Regulus' terms were "so severe" they would "brave anything" rather than submit, Polybius 1.31): if you press terms beyond what the war's balance supports, Carthage hires a Spartan general (Xanthippus, Polybius 1.32 to 1.34), a named man who gives its next battle a bonus. Victoria 3's main demand: ask for what you can get.

### 3g. Home, and getting back

- **Syracuse while you are away:** the keeper's loyalty check runs each season (commission switch), plots grow without you, and Carthage may besiege the city. Oath's Chancellor and exile: the man you leave may become the man you must remove.
- **Messages are slow:** news from Syracuse reaches you a season late, and your orders reach the keeper a season late (Agathocles learned of events in Sicily by ship, 20.33.1). Only while you are in Libya.
- **Going home:** sail home with the fleet. If you burnt it, you must take ships in Libya (a port town you hold gives one squadron a season) or **slip away alone**: Agathocles "embarked on the transport with a few followers and secretly sailed away", and "abandoned his sons", whom the soldiers slew; the army "made peace with the Carthaginians" (20.69.3). A big scene: you live, the army is gone, and any kin with it die.

### 3h. What to count

Per 300 games with everything on: how often bots cross; how often the crossing is caught; seasons in Libya; how often Carthage recalls its Sicilian army; how often the keeper betrays or Syracuse falls while you are away; how often you come home alone; how often peace is made from Libya. Broken: crossing wins almost always, or is never survivable. Bots need a "cross when Carthage's army is in Sicily and the keeper is loyal" plan to test it.

Carthage's forced debate and its three answers. 5. The siege of Carthage, terms and Xanthippus. 6. Home: keeper, slow news, slipping away. Each step is playable on its own.
