# Seat: the strategy video-game designer (council on units, contingents and ground, 2026-11-27)

Statement as returned by the seat (a separate AI session), condensed lightly. Its words; not a decision. Game mechanics are from the seat's memory unless listed as checked at the end.

## 1. Verdict
The draft is sound, and town contingents are the right centre: the big strategy games all tie troops to where they came from and how that place feels about you. It has a few too many small rules, some invented where a known game has a tested version (the "win by 3" ground bonus). Keep its shape, cut its side rules, borrow the proven versions.

## 2. Proposals
- **P1. Army = at most five named contingents, each from one home.** Inspired by: CK3 and Imperator (levies by holding); Mount & Blade: Bannerlord (an army of named lords' parties). History: 14.47.6. Medium.
- **P2. Heart read from home's lean, three marks: Steady, Wavering, Will leave.** Inspired by: CK3 clan vassals, whose levies scale with opinion (checked, ACOUP); Battle Brothers' morale ladder (Confident, Steady, Wavering, Breaking, Fleeing). Cheap.
- **P3. Wavering −1 to the army, capped at −2.** Inspired by: Total War's "Wavering" units fighting worse before they rout. Cheap.
- **P4. In a lost battle the worst-hearted contingent breaks first, takes the heaviest losses, its town hears.** Inspired by: CK2 flanks with their own commanders, routing separately; Total War's chain rout. History: 13.110.5-6, 13.112.3. Cheap.
- **P5. Leaving happens between seasons, warned a season ahead;** goes home, or over to Carthage if its army is in reach. Inspired by: Bannerlord army cohesion (lords ride off at zero); Warband desertion at low morale. History: 14.61.3, 14.58.1. Medium.
- **P6. Hostages hold a contingent.** History only (14.61.6); no confident game precedent. Cheap.
- **P7. Arms as one tag (heavy foot, horse, light troops, chariots for Carthage), mattering only through the ground; no counter table.** Inspired by: Imperator's per-type terrain modifiers, fewer here. Cheap.
- **P8. Contest the ground: d6 + general's skill (cap 2) + 1 for own land or local guides; the winner picks from the place's two or three grounds.** Inspired by: CK3 advantage from commander and terrain; De Bellis Antiquitatis (dice decide the invader, the defender lays terrain from his home land). History: 14.96.1. Small to medium.
- **P9. The loser of the ground roll pays the river penalty (−1 at a river); the winner never does.** Inspired by: EU4, where higher maneuver cancels the river-crossing penalty only (checked, Paradox forum). History: 13.109.5. Cheap. Replaces "win by 3".
- **P10. One standing wish: Open ground or Broken ground.** Used if you win the roll; otherwise Carthage's general's habit decides. Inspired by: Imperator's one tactic per battle; King of Dragon Pass and Six Ages battle scenes (exact options unsure). Cheap.
- **P11. Refusing battle costs a rearguard.** Lose the ground roll by 3+: fall back one stop; the worst-hearted contingent covers the retreat and takes losses, allies' hearts drop one mark. Inspired by: Warband, escaping by leaving troops behind (fairly sure). History: 14.61.3, 14.96.2. Cheap.
- **P12. Carthage's army as tokens with the same hearts, read from its blocs and silver.** Inspired by: Root, working on an opponent's weak parts (a loose parallel). History: 13.88.2, 14.75.6-9. Medium.

## 3. Cut or change
- Cut "win the ground roll by 3: +1 more" (double-counts the general); P9 instead.
- Merge "Keep to strong places" into refusing battle and Broken ground: two wishes plus a priced refusal.
- Cut "horse cannot be surprised by a night march". Horse: +1 on a plain, +1 in the ground roll (scouts), harder pursuit after a win.
- Move siege engines to a separate switch.
- Label "a wavering contingent that held turns steady, its town's lean rises" as our invention (good, keep).
- Cap at five; below about 15,000 men show one "allied levies" token.
- Keep the elephant line exactly.

## 4. Better precedents for the draft's inventions
Ground roll: DBA and CK3. Heart marks: Battle Brothers, Total War, Field of Glory II's cohesion states (Steady, Disrupted, Fragmented, Routed; fairly sure). Leaving: Bannerlord cohesion. Refusal's cost: Warband. Hiring beaten mercenaries: Mount & Blade recruiting prisoners (still parked with the mercenary market). Horsemen as the rich families: history (13.112.3, 14.64.5); nearest game parallel Imperator's legions loyal to their general. Park that loyalty idea for Leptines and the guard after 395.

## 5. Example battle (invented)
1. Your army at Gela, 25,000 men: the citizens' levy (steady), the guard (steady), the horsemen (wavering: the rich families fear your grain law), the Sicels (will leave next season: Himilcon's envoys are in their camp).
2. The ground: Himilcon knows these fields better, but luck is with you. You choose the broken hills above the river. Your Sicels like it; his chariots do not.
3. His men must cross the river to reach you (−1 to him). Your wavering horsemen hang back (−1 to you).
4. The line holds, and Carthage's Libyans give way. You win. The horsemen held, and the rich families grumble less.
5. In the night, the Sicels slip away to Himilcon's camp. You were warned last season.
