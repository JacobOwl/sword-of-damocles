# Seat: the historian (council on units, contingents and ground, 2026-11-27)

Statement as returned by the seat (a separate AI session), condensed. Its words; not a decision. The seat checked every quote on `docs/research/units-and-ground.md` (all accurate) and opened everything below in the saved texts; its new quotes are added to that card.

## 1. Verdict
- Contingents are the best-sourced part; armies in these wars broke along the lines of their towns and peoples again and again.
- The arms table and ground modifiers claim more than the sources show.
- The sources say much more about being **caught** (crossing a river, strung out, on a hill with no water) than about plain against hills. Build the ground rules around being caught.

## 2. Proposals
- **P1. A town fights best before its own walls, and won't march far from them.** A levy at its own town +1; wavers if its home is threatened while away. 13.60.4, 13.108.7, 13.110.4. Cheap.
- **P2. "Your home is in danger" pulls a contingent away, and a lie can trigger it.** A plate stratagem: a false report sends one enemy contingent home. Plutarch, Dion 27. Cheap.
- **P3. Replace the flat river −1 with being caught mid-crossing.** On river ground, winning the ground roll by 3+ catches the other side half across (only part fights); a close roll is a standoff. Timoleon 27, 16.79.5, 19.108.3-4, Dion 39; counter-case 13.87.1. Cheap.
- **P4. A hill with no water is a trap.** A beaten army may flee to a hill; next season it surrenders unless relieved; the victor chooses ransom, release or slaughter. Elleporus 389 (14.105.1-4), Cabala (15.15.3). Medium.
- **P5. Pursuit carries a risk.** Hard pursuit kills more (much more with horse on level ground), but an enemy reserve can catch a disordered pursuit; a loser that reaches its ships loses less. 13.60.6, 13.87.2, 19.109.4, 13.110.5. Cheap; fits thread 46's pursuit stage.
- **P6. When a general falls, his wing breaks** (existing deaths, not a commander per contingent). 15.17.1-2, 14.104.4, 15.15.3. Cheap.
- **P7. Mercenaries' hearts follow their pay; land is a way to pay.** Unpaid one season: waver; two: demand or go over. Settle them in a town as its new owners. 13.88.2, 16.78.5-16.79.1, 14.78.2-3, 14.15.3, 14.96.4, Entella 14.9.9. Medium.
- **P8. Greek mercenaries on both sides talk.** An event: the enemy general suspects his own and withdraws. Timoleon 20. Cheap.
- **P9. Carthage's citizen contingent is picked, rich and costly to lose;** if destroyed the council swears off risking citizens. 16.80.4, 16.81.3-4; Plutarch Timoleon 28 differs on the number. Cheap.
- **P10. A defeat spreads to nearby towns.** 14.88.5, 19.110.3-4, Plutarch Pyrrhus 23. Cheap; extends the built town-news rule.

## 3. Cut or change
- Cut "horse cannot be surprised by a night march": not in 13.88.1 or 13.109.3; Carthage had cavalry at Syracuse and was still surprised (14.62.3, 14.72.1).
- Horse +1 on a plain: keep small. Horse is shown screening, foraging, carrying news (13.54.3) and pursuing, never breaking infantry head-on. Put its value into pursuit and forage.
- Chariots −2 at a river overstates 16.80.2 (a rout in a hailstorm). The one described chariot action is a screen against horse on open ground (Timoleon 27); 200 chariots were lost at sea in 396 (14.55.3). Suggest: chariots cancel enemy horse on a plain, nothing more.
- The light-troops row is partly wrong: Campanians served as horse (13.44.2, 14.9.2) and storming foot (13.55.7); Sicels as light troops is invention. The hills-and-ambush bonus should follow local knowledge (14.96.1, 13.108.7), not troop type.
- Engines are not only Syracusan: Carthage's towers at Selinus (13.55.7), mining at Himera (13.59.8), rams at Gela (13.108.8).
- Carthage's citizens "rarely" understates it: 13.44.6, 15.15.2; Devereaux part 1. Say "sometimes".
- "The horsemen are the rich families" is an inference; label it. Diodorus shows the cavalry acting as one political body (13.112.3, 13.113.2-3, 14.7.7, 14.9.1).
- Card fixes: 13.62.5 is 409, not 405; Cronium is dated by Diodorus to Phanostratus' archonship (15.15.1, about 383/2), "about 375" is a modern redating (seat's dating note unverified).
- Refusing battle has a payoff too: the Chrysas waiting game ended in peace and Tauromenium (14.96.3-4).

## 4. Better precedents
- Hostages are half the story: the Campanians were held also because they "had sent their choicest troops to Syracuse" (14.61.6). Hostages can be handed back as a pretext for war (14.108.3).
- Distrusted men sent first: a second case, Hiero at the Cyamosorus (Polybius 1.9).
- "+1 for horse (scouts)" in the ground roll is weak (13.54.3 only); "+1 local ally" is solid (14.96.1).
- Elephants' dating holds (22.8.2, 22.10.2; Polybius 1.19). Polybius 1.30: Carthage's "best hopes rested on their cavalry and their elephants, and yet they abandoned the level plain" (Africa, 256, pattern only).

## 5. Example battle (invented)
1. Himilcon comes up the Gela road. You won the ground roll by 3: "Your Geloan scouts caught him at the river; a third of his army is still crossing."
2. Your army: the citizens' levy (steady), the guard (steady), Gela's levy (steady, +1: "they fight in sight of their own walls"), the Campanian horse (wavering, unpaid since spring).
3. His Libyans break on the bank. Then the turn: "The Campanian horse hang back; their pay is late."
4. You hold rather than pursue hard. "You remember Himera": his reserve on the far bank stays put.
5. After: Gela's lean rises. Camarina sends envoys. The Campanians ask for land at Leontini instead of silver.
