# Seat: the board-game designer (council on units, contingents and ground, 2026-11-27)

Statement as returned by the seat (a separate AI session), condensed lightly. Its words; not a decision. The seat opened the rulebooks it cites (links at the end); numbers are invention throughout.

## 1. Verdict
- Contingents are right, and board games have done them well: Sekigahara is close to a ready-made template for "5,000 from Gela who may backstab us".
- The draft makes desertion something you **read**; the better games make it something you **test** at the decisive moment, answered with a pledge you already hold. More readable and more dramatic.
- Ground has too many steps (roll, wish, margin bonus, refusal gate). Cut it to one roll and one card.

## 2. Proposals
- **P1. Contingents as stamped tokens with their home's emblem.** Inspired by: Sekigahara (every block carries its clan's mon: "The card used to deploy a block must have the same mon as the block"); Republic of Rome's legion counters. Cheap on top of A.
- **P2. The loyalty test.** If Carthage has an envoy on one of your wavering or ready-to-leave contingents, it calls a test when battle is joined. You keep it by showing a bond: hostages held, pay given this season, or its town's leading house your friend. No bond: it fights for Carthage in this battle, then goes home and its town leans one step toward Carthage. You can test Carthage's Sicels and Campanians the same way. Inspired by: Sekigahara's Loyalty Challenge (the owner keeps the block "by showing another card that could deploy it"; otherwise "the block turns sides"; it reverts after the battle). Passages: 14.58.1, 14.61.6. Change: the switch does not revert, because in the sources it does not. Medium.
- **P3. Envoy tokens, placed a season ahead and visible.** Carthage's bot puts one envoy on the worst-hearted contingent in reach, with a line saying so; you answer with a bond (hostages, pay, court the town). Shown face-up, not hidden as in Sekigahara, because a death must never feel random. Passage: Himilcon's offer to the Campanians (14.61.5-6). Cheap.
- **P4. Allies fight only near home.** A town levy is full strength in its own region; farther off (the march to Motya) one less, and it goes home at season's end unless paid. Inspired by: Hannibal: Rome vs Carthage (allies from political control "at the moment of the Land Battle", 14.2.1; independent allies "are not available outside of their own provinces", 14.2.3.3). Passage: 13.88.7. Cheap.
- **P5. The ground is one card from the place's small deck** (two or three cards, each one line who it favours and one who it hurts). Better general + d6 picks; otherwise the first card. Ground mostly **cancels** bonuses rather than adding: on hills horse and chariots give nothing; at a river the side that must cross gets −1. Inspired by: Commands & Colors: Ancients terrain, which caps dice ("Mounted units roll a maximum of 2 battle dice when battling an enemy unit that is downhill"). Passages: 13.109.5; 16.80.2 (later). Cheap to medium.
- **P6. Refusing battle is a roll, not a gate.** d6 + the gap in general skill: success, fall back one stop; failure, fight anyway at −1. Either way allied contingents waver. Inspired by: Hannibal: Rome vs Carthage's Avoid Land Battle (the seat opened the penalty for a failed avoid, not the exact procedure). Passages: 14.61.3, 14.96.2. Cheap.
- **P7. The beaten beast tramples its own side** (289 campaign only): a beaten elephant contingent gives its own army −1; chariots beaten at a river the same. Inspired by: Commands & Colors: Ancients' elephant Rampage ("An elephant, when it retreats, goes on a Rampage"). Passage: 16.80.2. Cheap.
- **P8. Veterans belong to their general.** A named general other than you who wins with the guard puts his marker on the guard token; its heart then reads his mood too. Inspired by: Republic of Rome ("The corresponding Legion Allegiance marker is placed on the Commander's card until he dies or the Legion is eliminated"; the rebel-following rule not opened, unverified). Touches rivals and offices: a switch, maybe `later.md`. Medium.
- **P9. Hearts on a three-step track beside the token.** Inspired by: Root's Vagabond relationship track (shape fairly sure, steps not). Cheap.

## 3. Cut or change
- Cut "wavering −1, capped at −2" (a hidden sum); replace with the P2 test. A wavering contingent with no envoy simply fights, and breaks first in a loss.
- Cut "win the ground roll by 3: +1 more".
- Two wishes only: seek open ground, seek broken ground ("Keep to strong places" overlaps "Hold the line").
- Refusing battle by P6's roll.
- "Ready to leave = leaves at season start" only through an envoy (P3) or the drift-home rule: two separate ways to vanish read as random.
- Keep: horsemen are the rich families; Carthage's contingents; no counter table; elephants only with Pyrrhus.

## 4. Better precedents
- Hostages: Sekigahara's "show a matching card to refute", the hostage being the card.
- The ground roll is our invention; Hannibal: Rome vs Carthage puts the general's Battle Rating into most rolls (interception, avoiding battle, resisting an elephant charge, 17.3.1). Use one skill number everywhere.
- Beaten elephants: Commands & Colors' Rampage.
- Worst-hearted breaks first: "in the spirit of" GMT's Great Battles of History (quality checks, army withdrawal level), not reopened, so unchecked.
- Losses by contingent: Sekigahara assigns losses to defected blocks first (8.7.2).
- Nothing confident from John Company, Time of Crisis, Pax Pamir, War of the Ring or Twilight Imperium for these rules.

## 5. Example battle (invented; uses the place and year of Gela 405 as a setting only)
1. Before: your army, the citizens (steady), the guard (steady), Gela's levy (wavering), the horsemen (wavering: the rich families are angry). An envoy of Himilcon sits with the horsemen.
2. Ground: Himilcon outmarched you (5 against 3) and draws *River and plain*: it favours horse and chariots; whoever must cross the river fights at −1. That is you.
3. The test: you hold no hostages from the rich families and have not paid them. The horsemen will not charge for you.
4. Clash: 4 + 3 = 7 against 5 + 4 = 9. Gela's levy breaks first and takes the heaviest losses. Gela will hear of it.
5. After: the horsemen ride off to Syracuse ahead of you, meaning to reach the city first. Warning: the rich families are under arms in your city (13.112.3).
