- **23 Guard dies only in battle: build, on, with a twist. Cheap.** Battle and storming hit guard and levy in proportion; hunger and plague hit the levy first; desertion from unpaid wages hits the guard first. In 404 the hired men didn't starve, they walked off: "constantly being abandoned by the mercenaries" (14.8.4). They held the Island, which he "gave to his friends and the mercenaries" (14.7.5).
- **24 Love slows the climb: build, on. Cheap.** A rival bound by marriage at opinion +2 or better can't reach rank 3 through his faction's agenda; his own steps still count. Hipparinus backed Dionysius (Aristotle *Politics* 1306a, via the cast card) and gave him Aristomache (14.44.8).
- **25 Retaking towns: build, on. Cheap.** Carthage leaves one hired unit behind; retaking needs an army (or a bribe). Sourced: "to the Campanians, whom Himilcon had left behind to guard the districts of Sicily" (14.8.5).
- **26 Carthage's quiet war: build, on, as diplomacy not an army. Medium.** The quiet is sourced: "after a slow recovery from the disaster" (14.95.1). Magon "formed alliances with most of the Siceli" (14.90.3), about 393 (a range, unchecked). Rule: while recalled, Carthage courts a town that dislikes you each season. The Libyan revolt rule stays invention (the English of 14.77 is not saved).
- **27 Mint: Strike coin side only. Cheap.** Back-to-back strikes cost one more point of the rich families' mood each time; resets after a season off. Keep the ally's +1. (Invention.)
- **28 Office requests: deal on, seat limit off. Medium.** Patronage was a trade: the best land "in gifts to his friends as well as to higher officers" (14.7.4). The CK3 cap is pure invention.
- **29 Pay in land: keep the fix, add the promise as a switch on in the test. Medium.** Leontini was his to give: "the city and territory of the Leontines" (14.78.2). Unkept, the mercenaries seize the nearest free town, as the Campanians took Entella and "possessed themselves of the city" (14.9.9).
- **30 Split. Medium.** Build ravaging (an army in enemy land lays waste the land; the town loses its next harvest): "ravaged the Carthaginian territory" (14.54.4). Supply limits are real: "the transport of his food supplies was reduced" (14.55.5). Park dragging and area foraging.
