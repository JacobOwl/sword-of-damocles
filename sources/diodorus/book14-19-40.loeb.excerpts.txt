14.40.1	The inhabitants of Rhegium, who were colonists of Chalcis, were angered to see the growing power of Dionysius.
14.40.1	For he had sold into slavery the Naxians and Catanians, their kinsmen,
14.40.1	and to the Rhegians, because they were of the same blood as these unfortunate peoples,
14.40.1	this act was the cause of no ordinary concern, since all feared the same disaster would befall them.
14.40.2	They therefore decided to take the field speedily against the tyrant before he became entirely secure.
14.40.2	Their decision upon war was forthwith supported strongly also by the Syracusans
14.40.2	who had been exiled by Dionysius, for most of them were at that time resident in Rhegium
14.40.2	and were continually discussing the matter and pointing out that all the Syracusans
14.40.2	would seize the occasion to join in an attack.
14.40.3	In the end the Rhegians appointed generals and sent out with them six thousand infantry
14.40.3	six hundred cavalry, and fifty triremes.
14.40.3	The generals crossed the strait and induced the generals of the Messenians to join in the war,
14.40.3	declaring that it would be a terrible thing for them to stand idly by when Greek cities,
14.40.3	and their neighbours, had been totally destroyed by the tyrant.
14.40.4	Now the generals were won over by the Rhegians and, without obtaining a vote of the people,
14.40.4	led forth their forces which consisted of four thousand infantry, four hundred cavalry, and thirty triremes.
14.40.4	But when the armaments we have mentioned had advanced as far as the borders of Messenê,
14.40.4	opposition broke out among the soldiers due to a harangue delivered by the Messenian Laomedon;
14.40.5	for he advised them not begin a war against Dionysius who had done them no wrong.
14.40.5	Accordingly the Messenian troops, since the people had not approved the war,
14.40.5	followed his advice at once, and, deserting their generals, turned back home;
14.40.6	and the Rhegians, since they were not strong enough alone for a battle,
14.40.6	when they saw that the Messenians were disbanding their army, also turned back speedily to Rhegium.
14.40.6	At the outset Dionysius had led out his army to the border of the Syracusan territory,
14.40.6	awaiting the attack of the enemy;
14.40.6	but when he learned of their retirement, he led his forces back to Syracuse.
14.40.7	When the Rhegians and Messenians sent ambassadors to treat upon terms of peace,
14.40.7	he decided that it was to his advantage to put an end to enmity against these states
14.40.7	and concluded peace.
